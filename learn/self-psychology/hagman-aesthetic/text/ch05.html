Chapter 5　The Creative Process（创作过程）

George Hagman · Chapter 5　The Creative Process（创作过程） · pp. 55–76 · 115 组英中对照 · 中文默认折叠

An area of human psychology where idealization and aesthetic experience combine is in the creative process, and most exquisitely in the creativity of the artist. Since Freud's (1908) early writings on the subject, the psychology of the artist has fascinated psychoanalysts. The analytic literature on art is large and varied, and each school of psychoanalytic thought has attempted to develop its own understanding of the artist and his or her work (for example, Ehrenzweig, 1967b; Freiberg, 1965; Freud, 1908, 1910, 1925a; Klein, 1929; Kris, 1952; Noy, 1979; Rank, 1932; Rose, 1992; Sachs, 1942; Segal, 1991; Stokes, 1963; Winnicott, 1971).

理想化与审美体验在人类心理中交汇的一个领域，就是创作过程[the creative process]，而其中最精微的形态则是艺术家的创造力。自弗洛伊德（1908）关于此主题的早期著作以来，艺术家的心理学一直令精神分析师着迷。关于艺术的分析文献既庞大又多样，每一精神分析思想学派都试图发展出自己对艺术家及其作品的独特理解（例如，Ehrenzweig, 1967b; Freiberg, 1965; Freud, 1908, 1910, 1925a; Klein, 1929; Kris, 1952; Noy, 1979; Rank, 1932; Rose, 1992; Sachs, 1942; Segal, 1991; Stokes, 1963; Winnicott, 1971）。

One crucial area of the psychology of the artist that has continually challenged and eluded analytic theorists is the psychodynamics of the creative process itself. In this regard, Freud (1925b) complained that, although his theory helped him understand the unconscious meanings of art, he despaired at unlocking the secrets of the creative act. In fact, subsequent analytic theories of creativity, while offering interesting observations, also failed to develop an adequate model of the psychological process that makes artistic creativity unique (Noy, 1979). In this chapter, I will show how contemporary psychoanalysis, and in particular self psychology, offer just such a model of the creative process.

艺术家的心理学中有一个关键领域，一直让分析理论家们不断受挫、无从把握，那就是创作过程本身的心理动力学[psychodynamics]。就此，弗洛伊德（1925b）曾抱怨说，尽管他的理论帮助他理解了艺术的无意识意义[unconscious meanings]，但他对解开创造活动[the creative act]的秘密仍感绝望。事实上，此后各种关于创造力的分析理论虽然提供了有趣的观察，却同样未能发展出一个足够的模型，来说明使艺术创造力成为独特的那个心理过程（Noy, 1979）。在本章中，我将表明，当代精神分析、尤其是自体心理学，恰恰提供了这样一个关于创作过程的模型。

I have developed the ideas presented in this chapter from several sources: First, my own experience as an artist, a vocation I pursued intensively for ten years and have continued to be involved with for the past twenty years; second, my knowledge of the experience of other artists, as patients and friends; third, a study of the writings of artists regarding their creativity; and fourth, a review of the psychoanalytic literature (particularly that of self psychology) on the creative process.

我在本章中提出的这些想法来自若干来源：第一，我自己作为艺术家的经验——这是我曾投入十年之久、并在过去二十年里持续参与的一种志业；第二，我从作为病人与朋友的其他艺术家那里获知的其经验；第三，对艺术家们关于自身创造力的著述的研究；第四，对关于创作过程的精神分析文献（尤其是自体心理学文献）的回顾。

My definition of the creative process is the following: the psychological processes of the artist that result in the creation of new, aesthetically legitimate works of art. I will not discuss the concept of creativity as a general psychological principle or as an aspect of our relationship to the world. It is not that I do not consider creativity in the broad sense to be important, or as something unrelated to the issue at hand; rather, I wish to focus on the creation of artworks. Is there a fundamental process that can account for the artist's ability to produce the extraordinary and sublime creations that we call "fine" art? Throughout this chapter, I will use the painter as my prototype of the artist for purposes of discussion and illustration; however, the perspective I take is, I believe, applicable to most forms of art, and perhaps to other forms of creative endeavors as well.

我对创作过程的定义如下：艺术家的那些心理过程，它们导致新的、在审美上站得住脚的艺术作品的创造。我不会把创造力当作一条普遍心理学原则、或当作我们与世界之关系的一个侧面来讨论。这并非因为我不认为广义的创造力重要，或认为它与眼下议题无关；而是因为我想聚焦于艺术作品的创造。是否存在一个根本性的过程，能够解释艺术家创造出我们称之为"纯"艺术["fine" art]的那些非凡而崇高的作品的能力？在本章中，为便于讨论与举例，我将以画家作为艺术家的原型；不过我相信，我所采取的视角适用于大多数艺术形式，或许也适用于其他形式的创造性活动。

As we have seen, classical drive psychologists emphasized the dreamlike nature of art. Freud himself (1908, 1910, 1925a) pioneered the application of dream interpretation and genetic reconstruction to the analytic study of art and artists. His focus was on the interpretation of artistic symbols and the uncovering of unconscious meanings. In fact, the interpretation of the creative process as a type of dreamwork became the primary focus of psychoanalysis for many decades. Albert Rothenberg (1979) describes the limits of this approach:

正如我们已看到的，经典驱力心理学家强调艺术的如梦性质。弗洛伊德本人（1908, 1910, 1925a）开创性地把梦的解析与发生学重建[genetic reconstruction]应用于对艺术与艺术家的分析性研究。他的关注点在于艺术象征[artistic symbols]的诠释以及对无意识意义的揭示。事实上，把创作过程解释为一种梦的工作[dreamwork]，在数十年间成了精神分析的首要焦点。阿尔伯特·罗森伯格[Albert Rothenberg]（1979）描述了这一进路的局限：

Although Freud was surely correct in suggesting that daydreaming or fantasy plays an important part in creation, he was incorrect in assuming that creative thinking consisted merely in disguising these fantasies in an acceptable form. (p. 130)¹

尽管弗洛伊德提出白日梦[daydreaming]或幻想在创造中扮演重要角色，这无疑是正确的；但他假定创造性思维仅仅在于把幻想伪装成一种可被接受的形式，这是错误的。 （p. 130）¹

Nonetheless, the notion that creativity has deep sources in unconscious processes is a profoundly important insight, which has direct implications for a self psychological model.

尽管如此，"创造力有着深植于无意识过程的源头"这一见解极为重要，它对自体心理学模型有直接的意涵。

With the ascendance of ego psychology, the emphasis began to shift to creativity as an activity of the ego (Kris, 1952; Sachs, 1942). Nonetheless, although there was an increasing focus on the role of the ego in art, creativity continued to be linked directly to the dynamics of the primary process, albeit now altered by defense mechanisms. For example, Louis Freiberg, in his paper, "New Views of Art and the Creative Process in Psychoanalytical Ego Psychology" (1965), summarized this model:

随着自我心理学的兴起，重心开始转向把创造力视为自我[ego]的一种活动（Kris, 1952; Sachs, 1942）。尽管如此，尽管人们越来越关注自我在艺术中的作用，创造力仍继续被直接联系于初级过程[primary process]的动力，只不过如今被认为受到防御机制的改造。例如，路易斯·弗赖伯格[Louis Freiberg]在其论文《精神分析自我心理学中关于艺术与创作过程的新观点》["New Views of Art and the Creative Process in Psychoanalytical Ego Psychology"]（1965）中总结了这一模型：

Creation may be characterized psychoanalytically as a process having two phases, inspiration and elaboration. In the first (inspiration), impulses from the Id attained a high degree of expression, but this occurs only under the close control of the ego that receives their powerful manifestations, shuts off the supply when it chooses, and turns them to its own uses. The artist builds upon the partial alteration of the fantasy, which has occurred unconsciously, but at least as significant a part of the work is conscious. The impulses, having achieved partial expression, are no longer in the position to exercise control, and the work of art may acquire greater independence from their demands, i.e., secondary autonomy. The elaborative phase of creation follows, and it is then that conscious relationships may be established. Connections are made, patterns are created, and communication is possible. A product emerges which is modified or even transformed into something, which can be received and understood by another person. The material is now subjected to an entirely different set of rules, the requirements of society, of communication, of art. (pp. 239–240)

从精神分析的视角看，创造可以被刻画为一个具有两个阶段的过程：灵感[inspiration]与精加工[elaboration]。在第一阶段（灵感），来自本我[Id]的冲动达到了高度的表达，但此事只有在自我的严密控制之下才发生——自我接收它们强有力的显现，在它选择时切断供给，并把它们转为己用。艺术家立足于幻想所发生的部分改变，而这一改变是无意识地发生的；但作品中至少有同等重要的一部分是有意识的。这些冲动既已获得部分表达，就不再处于能施加控制的位置，艺术作品也由此可以更大程度地独立于它们的要求，即获得次级自主性[secondary autonomy]。接着是创造的精加工阶段，正是在这时，有意识的关系得以建立。联结被做出，模式被创造，沟通成为可能。一个产物浮现出来，它被修改、甚至被转化为某种能够被他人接收和理解的东西。此时，材料被置于一套完全不同的规则之下——社会、沟通与艺术的诸要求。 （pp. 239–240）

According to ego psychologists such as Freiberg and Kris, the creative process involves a regression in the service of the ego (Kris, 1952) in which primitive drive impulses in the form of fantasies are consciously channeled and organized according to the rules of communication and art. To them, art is primarily a product of the secondary process acting upon primary process fantasies.

按照弗赖伯格与克里斯[Kris]这样的自我心理学家的看法，创作过程涉及一种 服务于自我的退行 [regression in the service of the ego]（Kris, 1952）：在其中，以幻想形式出现的原始驱力冲动，被按沟通与艺术的规则有意识地疏导和组织。对他们而言，艺术主要是次级过程[secondary process]作用于初级过程幻想之上的产物。

In an interesting variation of this model, Albert Rothenberg, in The Emerging Goddess (1979), viewed creativity as the mirror image of dreaming. Although appearing to have similar dynamics as primary process, the creative process begins and ends with consciousness and the drive for progressive discovery. Rather than channeling drive fantasies, the ego makes use of primary process thinking not as an end, but to an end. Thus, the creative ego moves freely between conscious and unconscious modes of thinking in pursuit of the exciting prospect of discovering something new and personally meaningful.

在这一模型的一个有趣变体中，阿尔伯特·罗森伯格在《正在显现的女神》[The Emerging Goddess]（1979）中把创造力视为梦的镜像。尽管看来具有与初级过程相似的动力，创作过程却始于并终于意识，以及那种追求步步推进的发现[progressive discovery]的驱力。自我并非在疏导驱力幻想，而是把初级过程思维不作为目的、而作为手段来使用。于是，创造性的自我在有意识与无意识的思维模式之间自由穿梭，追寻那发现某种新的、且对个人有意义之物的激动人心的前景。

The work of the ego psychologists, most especially that of Kris, contributed an important facet to the psychoanalytic model of creativity – that is, that the creative process involves a dynamic interaction between conscious and unconscious thought processes, between fantasy and reality. This is crucial, given that successful art must "work" according to conditions and terms that are radically different from those of the unconscious or asleep. Art exists for the real world, and ego psychologists understood this.

自我心理学家的工作，尤其是克里斯的工作，为创造力的精神分析模型贡献了一个重要方面——即创作过程涉及有意识与无意识思维过程之间、幻想与现实之间的动态交互。这一点至关重要，因为成功的艺术必须按照与无意识状态或沉睡状态截然不同的条件与法则"行得通"。艺术是为现实世界而存在的，自我心理学家们懂得这一点。

At the same time, from the perspective of object relations theory, Melanie Klein viewed creativity as a function of the psychological dynamics of the depressive position. In a model later extended by Hanna Segal, the Kleinians viewed the search for order and beauty as an attempt to reconstitute and preserve an internal good-object world, threatened with permanent disintegration because of the aggressive forces of the paranoid-schizoid position. What is important for us to note in this approach is the notion that creativity and art have a dynamic, integrative function in which the artist works to achieve a form of self-experience by means of creative activity. In his monograph In the Hidden Order of Art (1967b), Anton Ehrenzweig presented a three-stage model of the creative process, using a Kleinian framework:

与此同时，从客体关系理论的视角看，梅兰妮·克莱因[Melanie Klein]把创造力视为抑郁心位[the depressive position]之心理动力的功能。在后由汉娜·西格尔[Hanna Segal]加以扩展的模型中，克莱因学派把对秩序与美的寻求视为一种尝试：去重建并保存一个内在的善之客体世界[internal good-object world]——它因偏执-分裂心位[the paranoid-schizoid position]的攻击性力量而面临永久解体的威胁。在这一进路中，值得我们注意的是这样一个观念：创造力与艺术具有一种动态的、整合性的功能，艺术家在其中通过创造性活动努力获得某种形式的自体体验[self-experience]。在其专著《艺术的内在秩序》[In the Hidden Order of Art]（1967b）中，安东·艾伦茨威格[Anton Ehrenzweig]运用克莱因的框架，提出了创作过程的三阶段模型：

The creative process can thus be divided into three stages: an initial ("schizoid") stage of projected fragmented parts of the self into the work; unacknowledged split-off elements will then easily appear accidental, fragmented, unwanted, and persecutory. The second ("manic") phase initiates unconscious scanning that integrates arts substructure, but may not necessarily heal the fragmentation of the surface gestalt. In this stage all accidents seem to come right; all fragmentation is resolved. In the third stage of re-introjection part of the work's hidden substructure is taken back into the artist's ego on a higher level. Because the undifferentiated substructure necessarily appears chaotic to conscious analysis, the third stage too is beset with often-severe anxiety. Who has not experienced the gray feeling of the "morning after" when having to face the work done the day before? Part of the creative capacity is the strength to resist an almost anal disgust that would make us sweep the whole mess into the waste-paper basket. (pp. 102–103)

于是，创作过程可分为三个阶段：初始的（"分裂性"["schizoid"]）阶段，自体被投射的碎片性部分[projected fragmented parts of the self]进入作品；那些未被承认的、被分裂出去的元素[split-off elements]随后便容易显得偶然、破碎、不受欢迎且具迫害性。第二个（"躁狂性"["manic"]）阶段启动了无意识扫描[unconscious scanning]，它整合了艺术的深层结构[arts substructure]，但未必能治愈表层格式塔[surface gestalt]的碎裂。在这一阶段，一切偶然似乎都被纠正；一切碎裂都得到化解。在第三个再内摄[re-introjection]的阶段，作品隐藏的深层结构的一部分，被以更高的水平收回到艺术家的自我之中。由于未分化的深层结构对有意识的分析而言必然显得混乱，第三阶段同样常受严重焦虑的困扰。谁不曾体验过不得不面对前一天所做之工作时那种"次日清晨"["morning after"]的灰暗感受？创造能力的一部分，正是那种力量：抵抗一种近乎肛门性的厌恶[anal disgust]，这种厌恶会让我们把整个乱摊子扫进废纸篓。 （pp. 102–103）

In this model as described by Ehrenzweig, according to object relations theory, the dynamics of projective identification are applied to the creative process. I believe that the most relevant parts of the Kleinian model are: (1) the notion that aspects of self-experience become part of the artwork, and (2) a primary motive of creativity is reparation and the representation of the ideal. These points have direct relevance to self psychology, in that they presaged the notion of art as involving self-experience and the belief that the goal of creativity is the production of an experience of perfection and wholeness.

在艾伦茨威格所描述的这一模型中，按照客体关系理论，投射性认同 [projective identification]的动力被应用于创作过程。我认为克莱因模型中最相关的部分是：（1）自体体验的诸方面会成为艺术作品的一部分；（2）创造力的一个主要动机是修复[reparation]以及对理想的表现[the representation of the ideal]。这些要点与自体心理学直接相关，因为它们预示了"艺术涉及自体体验"这一观念，以及"创造力的目标是产生一种完美与整全[perfection and wholeness]的体验"这一信念。

On the other hand, D. W. Winnicott (1971), starting from but diverting greatly from the Kleinian viewpoint, proposed a special relationship between the inner world of the artist and the object of his or her creation. For Winnicott, an artwork can be understood as a type of transitional object, which is experienced by the artist as a part of the self, an object invested with fantasy. Creativity arises from the fluid relationship between the fantasy life of the artist and the plastic nature of the potential artwork. Creativity occurs in the transitional or potential space between the artist's inner fantasies and the world of objects. This is where the capacity to play is important.

另一方面，D. W. 温尼科特[D. W. Winnicott]（1971）从克莱因的观点出发、却又大大偏离了它，他提出艺术家的内在世界与其创造物之间有一种特殊关系。对温尼科特而言，艺术作品可以被理解为一种 过渡性客体 [transitional object]：艺术家把它体验为自体的一部分，一个被灌注了幻想的客体。创造力产生于艺术家的幻想生活与潜在艺术作品的可塑性质[plastic nature]之间那种流动的关系。创造力发生于艺术家的内在幻想与客体世界之间的过渡性空间或潜在空间[potential space]。正是在这里，游戏的能力[the capacity to play]显得重要。

Once again, for Winnicott, consistent with classical tradition, unconscious processes (fantasizing, in this case) impact on the artist's relationship with the world, altering the formal structure of reality to express subjective processes. Unfortunately, despite the richness of Winnicott's model, he does not differentiate between the creativity of the artist and the everyday creative engagement of an individual with his or her world. Given this limitation – although Winnicott offers us a stimulating view of human psychology – he remains disappointed by his failure to speak to the special quality found in the creation of art.

再一次地，对温尼科特而言——与经典传统相一致——无意识过程（在此情形中是幻想化）影响着艺术家与世界的关系，改变现实的形式结构以表达主体过程。遗憾的是，尽管温尼科特的模型十分丰富，他却没有区分艺术家的创造力与个体对其世界那种日常的创造性投入。鉴于这一局限——尽管温尼科特为我们提供了关于人类心理的引人入胜的看法——他仍对未能言及艺术创造中所见的那种特殊品质感到失望。

In 1980, Gilbert Rose published the first edition of his monograph The Power of Form (all quotes will be from the expanded 1992 edition), in which he offered a model of creativity as a distinct psychological process. Most important, Rose's work is a transition from the ego psychological and object relations perspectives toward a self psychological model of the creative process. This is due to the emphasis that Rose places on the role of narcissism in creativity. He wrote:

1980年，吉尔伯特·罗斯[Gilbert Rose]出版了他的专著《形式的力量》[The Power of Form]的第一版（本文所有引文均出自1992年的扩充版），他在其中提出了一个把创造力视为一种独特心理过程的模型。最重要的是，罗斯的工作标志着从自我心理学与客体关系视角向创作过程之自体心理学模型的过渡。这是由于罗斯对自恋[narcissism]在创造力中之作用的强调。他写道：

Unconscious remnants of the so-called omnipotent feelings of infancy can be seen to play an important role in the impulse to create. Whereas before, they were grandiose notions of the person the child thought him- or herself to be, they have become transformed into perfectionistic goals to which the child aspires to earn self-respect. The artist endows his or her work with this ego ideal of perfection. (p. 63)

所谓婴儿期全能感[omnipotent feelings]的无意识残余，可以被看到在创造的冲动中扮演着重要角色。它们从前是孩子对自身之所是的那些夸大的观念，如今则被转化为完美主义的目标，孩子渴望借此赢得自尊[self-respect]。艺术家则把这种关于完美的自我理想[ego ideal]赋予自己的作品。 （p. 63）

Echoing the Kleinian model, Rose believed that the artist is seeking to recover the experience of early states of perfection in which the child feels at one with an idealized, all-powerful mother. This is not to say that creativity implies psychopathology or developmental deficit. Rather, Rose (1992) believed that:

与克莱因模型相呼应，罗斯相信艺术家是在寻求重新获得早期完美状态的经验——那时孩子感到与一个被理想化的、全能的母亲合为一体。这并不是说创造力意味着精神病理或发展性缺陷。毋宁说，罗斯（1992）相信：

Everyman has undergone separation from what was the primordial, unitary pre-self, each of us is, so to speak, bereft of our original partner. Our imperfection, then, is our incompleteness, and seeking self-completion is the route (back) to perfection. (p. 67)

每个人都经历过与那个原初的、统一的前自体[pre-self]的分离；可以说，我们每个人都失去了自己最初的伴侣。于是我们的 不完美 [imperfection]，就是我们的 不完整 [incompleteness]；而寻求自我完成[self-completion]，就是通往（回归）完美的路。 （p. 67）

Unlike Klein, Rose did not think that aggression played a central role in creativity. To him, the loss of perfection formed a part of individuation and the establishment of self-experience.

与克莱因不同，罗斯不认为攻击性在创造力中扮演核心角色。对他而言，完美的丧失构成个体化[individuation]以及自体体验之确立的一部分。

The artist, according to Rose, is someone who turns inward to reexperience the early state of fusion, and thus to "gain an increased apprehension of the nature of reality" (p. 69). This is not just a private fantasy, but also an exploration of primitive fusion states of maternal oneness and bisexuality through which the artist "reshapes reality in new forms" (p. 69). Rose stated: "The re-emergence from narcissistic fusion and the re-establishment of ego boundaries carry the possibility of altered, perhaps even innovative, arrangements of the building blocks of reality" (p. 70). Rose continued:

在罗斯看来，艺术家是那种转向内在、去重新体验早期融合状态的人，并因此得以"对现实的本质获得更深的领会"（p. 69）。这不仅是一种私人的幻想，也是对与母亲合一及双性性[bisexuality]等原始融合状态的探索；艺术家正是借此"以新的形式重塑现实"（p. 69）。罗斯说："从自恋性融合中重新浮现、并重建自我边界，携带着重新安排现实之构成单元的可能性——这种安排或许是改变了的，甚至是创新的"（p. 70）。罗斯接着写道：

By making the work serve as a proxy, the artist can vicariously relive the primitive experiments of testing reality by repeated fusions and separations. The artwork is built up and melted down again and again, repeating second-hand the building up and melting down of psychic structure in the emergence from narcissicism. In this way the artist can give him- or herself up to the artwork, sometimes with the intensity of an addiction, and impress him- or herself upon it in repeated alternations of active mastery and passive surrender, of controlled fusion, letting go and recomposing control, to rediscover depths and limits. The artist resembles the earliest body imagery, perhaps in an unconscious fantasy of fusion and rebirth. The intensity of instinctual forces is reduced as self- and object representations become further refined, more internalization takes place, and, in the process, additional psychic structure is built from further drive neutralization. In other words, the artist searches for self-completion in the work; he or she has a private dialogue with a projected part of self-mirroring, smiling, frowning, approaching, and withdrawing, until the final completion and release. (p. 73)

通过让作品充当代理人[proxy]，艺术家能够以替代的方式重温那些通过反复融合与分离来检验现实的原始实验。艺术作品被一次次建构起来、又一次次融化下去，第二手地重复着从自恋中浮现时心理结构的建构与融化。以这种方式，艺术家可以把自己交付给作品，有时带着如成瘾般的强度，并在主动掌控与被动臣服、受控的融合、放手与重新调整控制之间反复交替，从而把自己印刻在作品之上，去重新发现深度与限度。艺术家类似于最早的身体意象[body imagery]，或许是在一种融合与重生的无意识幻想之中。随着自体表征[self-]与客体表征[object representations]变得更加精细、更多的内化发生，本能力量[instinctual forces]的强度也随之降低；在此过程中，进一步的驱力中性化[drive neutralization]又建构出额外的心理结构。换言之，艺术家在作品中寻求自我完成；他与一个被投射出去的自我的一部分进行私人对话——自我在那里映照、微笑、皱眉、靠近、退开——直到最终的完成与释放。 （p. 73）

Here Rose described how the artist engages in a dialectical process with the artwork, which is composed of the projected contents of the artist's psyche. In this way, the artist engages in an act of self-creation through structure building, both internal and external. Rose seemed to be describing a dialogue in which the artist creates the object as he creates his self. However, it is not just the artist's own self, but the universal human self-making process that is captured in the artwork.

在这里，罗斯描述了艺术家如何与艺术作品——它由艺术家心灵被投射的内容构成——进行一种辩证的[dialectical]过程。以这种方式，艺术家通过内部与外部两方面的结构建构，从事一种自我创造[self-creation]的行动。罗斯似乎是在描述一种对话：艺术家在创造客体的同时也创造着自体。然而，被艺术作品所捕捉的，不仅是艺术家自己的自体，而且是那普遍的人类自我塑造[universal human self-making]过程。

The major forms of creative imagination transcend the private and restructure reality itself. A work of art simplifies but also expands and deepens the view of the world and of the self. As the ego's coordinated activity recapitulates the past in the light of the present to prepare for the future, the artwork summarizes and magnifies the process by which each of us continually tests and master reality, relating inner and outer in repeated fusions and separations. The creative work that remains behind represents the cast of a mind "reborn" and objectified in the process of thought, feeling and action. (pp. 77–78)

创造性想象的主要形态超越私人性，并重塑现实本身。一件艺术作品既简化、也扩展并深化我们对世界与自体的看法。正如自我的协调性活动以当下之光重演过去、为未来作准备，艺术作品也概括并放大我们每个人持续检验并掌控现实、在反复的融合与分离中联结内在与外在的那个过程。留存下来的创作成果，代表着一种"重生"并被客体化的心灵之模铸——它在思维、感受与行动的过程中成形。 （pp. 77–78）

While Rose was publishing his psychoanalytic aesthetics, Heinz Kohut (1971, 1977) was reworking the psychoanalytic theory of narcissism and elaborating it beyond the classical model that Rose relied on. It is certainly beyond the scope of this chapter to compare Kohut's self psychology and the classical understanding of narcissism. However, I suggest that it may be interesting to keep Rose's viewpoint in mind (based as it was on the metapsychological system, which Kohut initially worked with) as we look at the self psychological perspective.

当罗斯在发表他的精神分析美学时，海因茨·科胡特[Heinz Kohut]（1971, 1977）正在重铸精神分析的自恋理论，并把它推进到超出罗斯所依凭的经典模型之外。对科胡特的自体心理学与经典的自恋理解加以比较，当然超出本章的范围。不过我认为，当我们审视自体心理学视角时，把罗斯的观点（它奠基于元心理学体系[metapsychological system]，而科胡特最初正是从这个体系出发的）记在心里，或许会很有意思。

Self psychology has tried to identify the special role of self-experience in the creative process. Kohut felt that the creative person possesses a more fluid self-structure, characterized by dynamically active, archaic modes of psychological organization. He believed that the artist's relationship to the world was narcissistically driven and that the boundaries between self and object were less rigid:

自体心理学试图指认自体体验在创作过程中的特殊作用。科胡特认为，有创造力的人拥有一种更为流动的自体结构[self-structure]，其特征是心理组织具有动力上活跃的、古老的[archaic]模式。他相信艺术家与世界的关系是自恋性地被驱动的，而且自体与客体之间的边界不那么僵硬：

In creative work narcissistic energies are employed which have been changed into a form to which I referred earlier as idealizing libido. The creative individual is less separated from his surroundings than the non-creative one; the "I–You" barrier is not as clearly defined. The creative individual is keenly aware of these aspects of his surroundings that are of significance to his work, and he invests them with narcissistic-idealizing libido. (1966, p. 112)

在创造性工作中，被使用的是那些已经转变成我先前所称的"理想化力比多"[idealizing libido]这一形式的自恋能量。有创造力的个体与其周遭环境的分离程度低于无创造力的个体；"我—你"["I–You"]的界线没有那么清晰。有创造力的个体敏锐地觉察到其周遭环境中那些对他的工作具有意义的方面，并以自恋-理想化力比多[narcissistic-idealizing libido]投注于其上。 （1966, p. 112）

At the heart of creativity, Kohut believed, the artist seeks an experience of perfection, or rather the reexperiencing of a lost, ideal self-state:

科胡特相信，在创造力的核心，艺术家寻求的是一种完美的体验，或者更确切地说，是对一种已丧失的理想自体状态[ideal self-state]的重新体验：

Creative artists may be attached to their work with the intensity of an addiction, and they try to control and shape it with forces and for purposes, which belong to the narcissistically experienced world. They are attempting to re-create a perfection that formerly was directly an attribute of their own. (1985, p. 115)

创造性的艺术家可能以成瘾般的强度依恋于自己的作品，并且试图以属于自恋性地被体验的世界的力量和目的去控制并塑造它。他们试图重新创造一种完美——这种完美从前直接是他们自身的一种属性。 （1985, p. 115）

Kohut envisioned a form of psychological process that characterizes the internal psychological life of the artist. This process involves movement from states of self-cohesion to fragmentation and depletion, to periods during which the self can reinvest the work with renewed energy. He elaborated these ideas as follows:

科胡特构想了艺术家内在心理生活所特有的一种心理过程形式。这一过程涉及从自体凝聚[self-cohesion]状态，走向碎裂[fragmentation]与耗竭[depletion]，再走向自体能够以更新的能量重新投注于作品的时期。他如下阐述了这些想法：

The psychic organization of some creative people is characterized by a fluidity of the basic narcissistic configurations, i.e., that periods of narcissistic equilibrium (stable self-esteem and securely idealized internal values; steady, persevering work characterized by attention to details) are followed by (precreative) periods of emptiness and restlessness (decathexis of values and low self-esteem; addictive perverse yearnings: no work), and that these, in turn, are followed by creative periods (the unattached narcissistic cathexis which had been withdrawn from the ideals and from the self are now employed in the service of the creative activity: original thought, intense passionate work). A phase of frantic creativity (original thought) is followed by a phase of quiet work (the original ideas of the preceding phase are checked, ordered and put into communicative form, e.g., written down), and that this phase of quiet work is in turn interrupted by a fallow period of precreative narcissistic tension, which ushers in a phase of renewed creativity, and so on. (1977, p. 815)

一些有创造力之人的心理组织，其特征是基本自恋构型[basic narcissistic configurations]具有流动性，即：自恋平衡时期（稳定的自尊与稳固地被理想化的内在价值；以关注细节为特征的稳定、坚忍的工作）之后，是（前创造期的[precreative]）空虚与躁动时期（价值去投注[decathexis]与低自尊；成瘾性的悖常渴求[addictive perverse yearnings]：无法工作），而这些时期又接着被创造期所跟随（那些已从理想和自体撤回的、无所依附的自恋投注，如今被用于创造性活动：原创的思想、强烈而充满激情的工作）。一个狂热的创造阶段（原创思想）之后，是一个安静工作的阶段（前一阶段的原创想法被核对、整理，并被置入可沟通的形式，例如写下来）；而这个安静工作的阶段又反过来被一个前创造期自恋张力的休耕期[fallow period]所打断，由此引入一个重新创造的阶段，如此等等。 （1977, p. 815）

At the present time, we understand this process as an unfolding experience of self-cohesion, selfobject failure, restoration, and renewed self-experience. Kohut's model of creativity stresses the relationship between selfobject failure and restoration at the heart of the model; later, I will further develop this idea.

在目前，我们把这一过程理解为一种逐步展开的体验：自体凝聚、自体客体失败[selfobject failure]、修复[restoration]，以及更新了的自体体验。科胡特的创造力模型强调自体客体失败与修复之间的关系，并将其置于模型的核心；稍后我将进一步展开这一想法。

Charles Kligerman was the first to devote an entire paper to the development of a self psychology of creativity. Essentially in agreement with Kohut, Kligerman (1980) described four characteristics of creativity:

查尔斯·克利格曼[Charles Kligerman]是第一位以整篇论文致力于发展创造力之自体心理学的人。克利格曼（1980）在根本上与科胡特一致，他描述了创造力的四个特征：

An intrinsic joy in creating. This is perhaps the most important factor, but the one we know least about. The exhibitionistic, grandiose ecstasy of being regarded as the acme of beauty and perfection and the nearly insatiable need to repeat and confirm this feeling. The need to regain a lost paradise – the original bliss of perfection – to overcome the empty feeling of self-depletion and to recover self-esteem. In the metapsychology of the self, this would amount to healing the threatened fragmentation and restoring firm self-cohesion through a merger with the self-object – the work of art – and a bid for mirroring approval of the world. We can also add a fourth current to the creative drive – the need to regain perfection by merging with the ideals of the powerful selfobjects, first the parents, then later revered models that represent the highest standards of some great artistic tradition. (pp. 387–388)

创造中一种内在的喜悦。这或许是最重要的因素，却也是我们了解最少的。 被视作美与完美之极致时所体验的那种展示性的、夸大的狂喜[exhibitionistic, grandiose ecstasy]，以及近乎无法餍足的、要重复并确认这一感受的需要。 需要重获一个失去的乐园——原初的完美之至福——以克服自体耗竭[self-depletion]的空虚感，并恢复自尊。在自体的元心理学中，这相当于通过与被自体-客体[self-object]——即艺术作品——的融合，以及向世界争取镜像性的认可[mirroring approval]，来治愈受威胁的碎裂并恢复稳固的自体凝聚。 我们还可以为创造驱力加上第四股潮流——需要通过与被强有力自体客体的理想相融合来重获完美，这些自体客体先是父母，后来则是代表着某种伟大艺术传统之最高标准的、受尊崇的典范。 （pp. 387–388）

Kligerman's paper opened the way to a new perspective on creativity, in which the artist actively seeks to bring about an experience to confirm and/or repair his or her sense of self. Rather than a regressive phenomenon, creativity is a complex, high-level psychological activity that attempts to create an experience in which a fragile or precarious self is linked to an ideal object, to idealized relationships, and to ideal values. As a result of the successful creative effort, the artist experiences him- or herself as renewed, confirmed, and vitalized. Kligerman's psychology of the artist is based on a model of developmental psychopathology; however, he suggests that the drive to create may occur without serious self pathology, and that it may be engaged in as an end.

克利格曼的论文开启了一条通往创造力新视角的道路：在其中，艺术家主动寻求带来一种体验，以确认和/或修复他的自体感[sense of self]。创造力不是一种退行现象，而是一种复杂的、高层次的心理活动；它试图创造这样一类体验：在其中，一个脆弱或不稳固的自体被联结于一个理想客体、理想化的关系与理想的价值。作为成功的创造性努力的结果，艺术家体验到自己被更新、被确认并充满活力。克利格曼关于艺术家的心理学奠基于发展性精神病理学[developmental psychopathology]的模型；不过，他指出创造的驱力也可能在没有严重自体病理的情况下出现，并且它可以作为一种目的本身[as an end]而被投入。

In 1988, Carl Rotenberg sought to develop a more elaborate self psychological model of creativity. Rather than viewing art from a purely clinical perspective, Rotenberg recognized the "powerful organizing influence that visual arts have for the self" (p. 195). Focusing primarily on the viewer of art, he argued for the important role of creativity and art appreciation in normal human psychology. For example, consistent with Kohut's viewpoint that selfobjects play a role not just in an archaic developmental sense, but throughout life – even in the psychological lives of emotionally healthy adults – Rotenberg noted that both the artist and the viewer seek out the confirming, vitalizing, and transformative functions of the aesthetic selfobject experience. He believed that they do so not to cope with pathology, but rather as a healthy, affirmative, and vital experience in and of itself.

1988年，卡尔·罗滕伯格[Carl Rotenberg]试图发展出一个更详尽的自体心理学创造力模型。罗滕伯格不是从纯粹的临床视角来看待艺术，而是认识到"视觉艺术对自体所具有的强大组织性影响"（p. 195）。他主要聚焦于艺术的观看者，主张创造力与艺术欣赏在正常人类心理中具有重要地位。例如，与科胡特的观点——自体客体不只在古老的发展意义上发挥作用，而是贯穿一生，甚至见于情感健康成人的心理生活中——相一致，罗滕伯格指出，艺术家与观看者都在寻求审美自体客体体验[aesthetic selfobject experience]所具有的确认、赋予活力与转化的功能。他相信，他们这样做不是为了应对病理，而是把它当作一种健康的、肯定性的、本身即具活力的体验。

In addition, Rotenberg saw the selfobject function of art as embedded in a shared experiential space between the subjectivity of the viewer and the artist. The point at which these multiple subjectivities come together, of course, is the experience of the artwork itself. The question is in what sense the artwork contains or reflects the subjectivity of the artist. In this regard, Rotenberg (1988) had something quite interesting to say:

此外，罗滕伯格认为艺术的自体客体功能，镶嵌于观看者的主体性与艺术家的主体性之间的一个 共享体验空间 [shared experiential space] 之中。这些多重主体性汇合之处，当然就是艺术作品本身的体验。问题在于：艺术作品在何种意义上包含或反映了艺术家的主体性？就此，罗滕伯格（1988）说了些相当有意思的话：

[The artist] puts his own puzzles and mental ambiguities outside of himself and then reacts to them as if they were other than his. In a sense, once the artist begins a work, he surrenders to it as though the work were dominating him, demanding the solution of its own ambiguities, and requiring completion. The artist experiences the selfobject functioning of the artwork as alive, active, interpretive and eventually having transformational capabilities, to the extent that inner puzzles of the artist are worked out through this externalization. (p. 209)

[艺术家]把他自己的谜题与心智上的含混放置到自身之外，然后对它们的反应就好像它们并非属于他。在某种意义上，艺术家一旦开始一件作品，他就向它投降，仿佛作品在支配着他，要求解决它自身的含混，并要求被完成。艺术家把艺术作品的自体客体功能体验为活的、主动的、具有诠释性的，并最终具有转化能力——其程度至于艺术家的内在谜题正是通过这种外化[externalization]得以解决。 （p. 209）

Thus, Rotenberg had begun to identify the internal dynamics of the creative process, though not in the traditional terms of intrapsychic life; rather, he was talking about the dialectical relationship between the artist and evolving artwork, in which aspects of the artist's subjectivity are externalized as artistic form.

由此可见，罗滕伯格已开始指认创作过程的内在动力，不过不是以心理内部生活的传统术语；毋宁说，他谈论的是艺术家与不断演化的作品之间的辩证关系——在其中，艺术家主体性的诸方面被外化为艺术形式。

Here I would like to elaborate on Rotenberg's model. Rather than seeing the artist as surrendering to the work, my view is that the artist engages in a process in which internal and externalized aspects of self-experience enter a dialectical relationship that transforms both. Successful creation is determined by the symbolic articulation of feeling, of the vital experience of living made manifest through the completed artwork.

在此我想对罗滕伯格的模型加以展开。与把艺术家视为向作品投降的看法不同，我的观点是：艺术家参与的是一个过程，在其中，自体体验的内部方面与外化方面进入一种辩证关系，而这种关系会转化二者。成功的创造，取决于感受[feeling]、以及那活生生的生命体验通过完成的作品得以彰显的象征性表达[symbolic articulation]。

Let me add that Suzanne Langer's (1953, 1957a, 1957b) aesthetic theory is highly compatible with principles of self psychology, particularly as it incorporates her view of art as the externalization of subjective experience. Examining Langer's theory allows us to see more clearly the unique nature of the aesthetic selfobject experience, where the artist is linked not to another object per se, but to externalized aspects of his or her own subjective life.

我还要补充：苏珊·朗格[Suzanne Langer]（1953, 1957a, 1957b）的美学理论与自体心理学的原则高度相容，尤其是因为她把艺术视为主观体验之外化的观点。考察朗格的理论，能让我们更清楚地看到审美自体客体体验的独特性质——在其中，艺术家所联结的并不是另一个客体本身[per se]，而是他自己主观生活中被外化的诸方面。

Creativity and Selfobject Experience（创造力与自体客体经验）

Art brings together the real and the perfect. (Stokes, 1963, p. 26)

艺术把真实之物与完美之物带到一起。 （Stokes, 1963, p. 26）

Although art is fundamentally everywhere and always the same, nevertheless, two main human inclinations appear in its many and varied expressions. One aims at the direct creation of universal beauty, the other at the aesthetic expression of oneself, in other words, of that which one thinks and experiences. (Mondrian, 1937, p. 561)

尽管艺术在根本上无处不在、且始终同一，但在其众多而多样的表现中，人的两种主要倾向显现出来。一种旨在直接创造普遍的美，另一种则旨在对自身的审美表达——换言之，即表达一个人所思所感之物。 （Mondrian, 1937, p. 561）

In discussing the creative process, it is important to understand just how the artwork is linked to the artist's self-experience, both in process as well as in completed form. What I would like to explore is the following question: "If the artwork exists in a borderland or transitional area between the artist's inner world and reality, what is the nature of that relationship?" I think that part of the answer can be found in Langer's work on creativity and symbolism, in which she argues that art is a type of language of human experience, of human feeling.

在讨论创作过程时，重要的是理解艺术作品究竟如何与艺术家的自体体验相连——无论就过程而言，还是就完成的形式而言。我想探讨的是下面这个问题："如果艺术作品存在于艺术家的内在世界与现实之间的边境地带或过渡性区域，那么这一关系的性质是什么？"我认为，答案的一部分可以在朗格关于创造力与象征[creativity and symbolism]的著作中找到；她在其中主张，艺术是一种人类经验、人类感受的语言。

Langer developed a remarkable and influential theory of art, in which a new view of symbolization explains the nature of artistic expression. She suggested that art symbolizes "forms of feeling," and that there is a parallel between different arts and common forms of human feeling. And for Langer, feeling means far more than emotion. In Problems of Art (1957), Langer argued that art is fundamentally different from discursive forms of symbolism, such as written language; rather, it is how artists capture and communicate lived experience. According to Langer (1957):

朗格发展出一种卓越而有影响的艺术理论，其中一种关于象征化[symbolization]的新看法解释了艺术表达的性质。她提出，艺术所象征的是"感受的形式"["forms of feeling"]，并且不同的艺术门类与人类感受的共同形式之间存在一种平行关系。而对朗格来说，感受 [feeling] 一词的含义远不止于情绪[emotion]。在《艺术问题》[Problems of Art]（1957）中，朗格主张艺术与书写语言这类推论性象征形式[discursive forms of symbolism]有根本区别；艺术毋宁是艺术家捕捉并传达其活生生经验[lived experience]的方式。按朗格（1957）所说：

An artist expresses feeling, but not in the way a politician blows off steam or a baby laughs and cries. He formulates that elusive aspect of reality that is commonly taken to be amorphous and chaotic; that is, he objectifies the subjective realm. A work of art expresses a conception of life, emotion, and inward reality. But it is neither a confessional, nor a frozen tantrum; it is a developed metaphor, a non-discursive symbol that articulates what is verbally ineffable – the logic of consciousness itself. (p. 26)

艺术家表达感受，但不是以政客发泄情绪、或婴儿又笑又哭的方式。他所赋形的是现实中被通常认为无形而混乱的那个难以捉摸的方面；也就是说，他把主体领域客体化。一件艺术作品表达一种关于生命、情绪与内在现实的构想。 但它既不是一场自白，也不是一阵凝固的发作；它是一个发展成熟的隐喻，一个非推论性的象征[non-discursive symbol]，它表达出在言语上无法言喻之物——意识本身的逻辑。 （p. 26）

What does it mean to express one's idea of some inward or "subjective" process? It means to make an outward image of this inward process for oneself and others to see; that is, to give the subjective events an objective symbol. It is an outward showing of inward nature, an objective presentation of subjective reality. It is the created image that has elements and patterns like the life of feeling. But this image, though it is a created apparition, a pure appearance, is objective; it seems to be charged with feeling because its form expresses the very nature of feeling. Therefore, it is an objectification of subjective life. (p. 9)

表达一个人关于某种内在或"主观"过程的想法，意味着什么？它意味着为这一个内在过程造出一个外显的意象[image]，使自己与他人得以看见；也就是说，赋予那些主观事件一个客观的象征。它是对内在本性的一种外显展示，是对主观现实的一种客观呈现。那被创造出来的意象，其要素与模式如同感受的生命。然而这一意象尽管是一种被创造的幻象[apparition]、一种纯粹的显象[appearance]，却是客观的；它似乎被感受所充溢，因为它的形式表达的正是感受的本性。因此，它是主观生命的客体化[objectification]。 （p. 9）

Langer believed that the articulation of feeling is not the same as emotional expression, affective or cathartic, although these components of experience may be used as raw material for the creation of artistic form. She stated that in art, "we don't want self-expression" (1957b, p. 25). In artistic creation, the artist attempts to objectify by means of symbolization the fullness of lived experience – the feeling not simply of emotion or affect, but of life itself. The following quotation from the nonrepresentational artist Robert Motherwell expresses some of what Langer tried to say:

朗格相信，感受的表达不等同于情绪表达、情感表达或宣泄性表达，尽管这些经验成分可以被用作创造艺术形式的原材料。她说，在艺术中，"我们并不想要自我表达"["we don't want self-expression"]（1957b, p. 25）。在艺术创造中，艺术家试图借助象征化，使自己活生生经验的整全客体化——那感受不只是情绪或情感，而是生命本身。下面这段引自非具象艺术家罗伯特·马瑟韦尔[Robert Motherwell]的话，表达了朗格试图表达的部分内容：

I never think of my paintings as "abstract," nor do those who live with them day by day. I happen to think primarily in paint – this is the nature of the painter – just as musicians think in music. And nothing can be more concrete to man than his own felt thought, his own thought feeling. I feel most real to myself in the studio and resent any description of what transpires there as "abstract," something remote from reality. (Motherwell as quoted by Proter, 1997, p. 256)

我从不把自己的画作想成"抽象的"，那些每日与它们共处的人也不这样想。我碰巧主要以颜料思考——这就是画家的本性——正如音乐家以音乐思考。对人来说，没有什么比他自己被感受到的思想、他自己的思想感受更为具体。在工作室里，我感到自己最为真实；我反感把那里发生的一切描述为"抽象的"、某种远离现实的东西。 （Motherwell，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 256）

For Motherwell and Langer, then, the artist's self-experience is made real to him or her through the creative process, and this experience is only partly affective. Langer argued that those emotions that we commonly identify as anger, sadness, or joy are the most obvious and socially delimited aspects of inner life. The life of feeling, the experience of self in the world, is, according to Langer, largely ineffable and incommunicable in logical forms, such as linguistic discourse (see also Kohut's [1977] description of the unknown self). However, self-experience can be contained and communicated through the formal relations of art – in space, movement, and/or the dynamic relations of interacting forms.

那么，对马瑟韦尔与朗格而言，艺术家的自体体验正是通过创作过程而对他自己变得真实的，而这种体验只有一部分是情感性的。朗格主张，那些我们通常辨识为愤怒、悲伤或喜悦的情绪，是内在生活中最显见、且被社会界定得最清楚的那些方面。按朗格所说，感受的生命、自体在世界中的体验，在很大程度上是无法言喻的，也无法以逻辑形式（例如语言论述）来传达（另见科胡特[1977]对未知的自体[the unknown self]的描述）。然而，自体体验可以通过艺术的形式关系——在空间、运动，以及/或交互形式的动力关系之中——被容纳并传达。

The artist, through the process of creation, seeks to find objective expression of the experience of living – in other words, his or her subjectivity. The artist attempts to articulate the fullness of self-experience. In this sense, self-experience is not found in a momentary experience of joy, or in grief or anger (although these affects may have their role). Art does not simply capture how we feel; it articulates whom each of us is: a living person with an inner life, having its own rhythms and connections, crises and breaks, complexity and richness. However, more important, art expresses subjectivity through formal perfection and the realization of ideal values. Kohut (1985) saw this as an important aspect of the creative process:

艺术家通过创造的过程，试图为活着的体验找到客观的表达——换言之，为他自己的主体性找到客观表达。艺术家试图表达自体体验的整全。在这个意义上，自体体验并不存在于某个瞬间的喜悦、悲伤或愤怒之中（尽管这些情感或许有它们的作用）。艺术并不只是捕捉我们感受如何；它表达我们每个人是谁：一个拥有内在生命的活人，有着自己的节律与联结、危机与断裂、复杂与丰富。然而更重要的是，艺术通过形式的完美与理想价值的实现来表达主体性。科胡特（1985）把这一点视为创作过程的一个重要方面：

A leading part of the psychological equipment of creative people has been shaped through the extensive elaboration of a transitional point in libido development: idealization. (p. 114)

有创造力之人的心理装备中，有一主要部分是通过力比多发展中一个过渡点——即理想化——的广泛展开而被塑形的。 （p. 114）

Through art, what is temporary and ineffable is expressed in terms that are permanent (even eternal), vivid, and beautiful. What is common becomes sublime. What is incomplete becomes whole. This is crucial to our understanding of creativity: the artist does not simply express feeling; rather, feeling must be expressed in an ideal form. It is the accomplishment of an idealized formal organization that gives cohesion, vitality, and continuity to the aesthetic experience, and thus to the self-experience of the artist and, by extension, to the experience of his or her audience.

通过艺术，那些短暂而无法言喻之物，被以永久（甚至永恒）、鲜明而美的辞语表达出来。凡庸变成了崇高。不完整变成了整全。这对我们理解创造力至关重要：艺术家并不只是表达感受；毋宁说，感受必须被以理想的形式表达出来。正是理想化的形式组织之达成，赋予了审美体验——并因而赋予艺术家的自体体验，进而延及他受众的体验——以凝聚、活力与连续。

What we have been discussing regarding creativity was also described by Kohut in more general terms in his psychology of the self. For example, optimal self-experience for Kohut involved feelings of self-cohesion, self-continuity, and vitality; he did not intend to promote a bland uniformity, however, but rather a vital, dynamic inner world of feeling and fantasy. The sense of self-continuity is not just of ongoing sameness; it is the meaningful flow of lived experience. And vitality is not just excitement; it is the experience of being fully and clearly alive to oneself, to other people, and to the world. Kohut described how this cohesive, continuous, and vital experience of a person's self-originated and was then maintained and/or repaired through selfobject experiences.

我们一直在讨论的关于创造力的内容，科胡特也在其自体心理学中以更一般性的说法加以描述。例如，对科胡特而言，最优的自体体验涉及自体凝聚、自体连续感[self-continuity]与活力[vitality]的感受；然而他并不想提倡一种温吞的一致，而是要提倡一个充满活力、动态的内在感受与幻想世界。自体连续感并不只是始终如一[ongoing sameness]；它是活生生经验的有意义的流动。而活力也不只是兴奋；它是向自己、向他人、向世界完整而清晰地活着的体验。科胡特描述了这种凝聚、连续而有活力的体验，如何起源于一个人的自体，随后又通过自体客体体验得以被维持和/或修复。

Put simply, selfobject experience arises because of engagement with another, and the experience of that engagement results in a psychological state of greater self-cohesion, continuity, and/or vitality. In most instances, selfobject experience is associated with relationships with other people, but Kohut was also clear that other objects, institutions, and even ideas could serve the same function. However, the selfobject experience is most significantly a transcendent one. The self is felt to be grand, the other ideal, and the companionship of the twin sublime.

简单来说，自体客体体验之所以产生，是因为与另一个他者的投入[engagement]；而这种投入的体验，造成了更大的自体凝聚、连续性和/或活力这一心理状态。在多数情形中，自体客体体验与和他人的关系相关联；但科胡特也明确表示，其他客体、机构，甚至观念，都能起到同样的功能。然而，自体客体体验最显著地是一种超越性[transcendent]的体验。自体被感受为宏大的，他者被感受为理想的，而孪生者[twin]的陪伴则是崇高的。

Aesthetic creativity is motivated by the desire to create selfobject experience through artistic activity. In this regard, artistic creation is a unique form of relatedness. The artist creates an object, which, as previously noted, is a formal embodiment of his or her deepest experience of being in the world. The artist's relationship to the art object is a relationship with his or her own subjectivity. Not only does the artist create an ideal object, mirroring the grandiosity of the artist's self-experience, but more important to our purposes, the artist seeks that perfection through a dialectical process between self-experience and the experience of the art object, which are both merged and separate, and in which split aspects of self-experience alternately blend and differentiate. For the artist, it is never enough to sit and enjoy the fruit of his or her labor; the artist is compelled again and again to re-create a process of self-experiencing in which he or she seeks an elusive yet powerful affective event. Once the object is finished, possessing a fully separate life, the artist must return to the process, the continuing search.

审美创造力的动力，来自通过艺术活动创造自体客体体验的愿望。就此而言，艺术创造是一种独特的关联形式。艺术家创造出一个客体，如先前所指出的，这个客体是他对自己在世界中存在着这一最深刻体验的形式化体现。艺术家与艺术客体的关系，是一种与他自身主体性的关系。艺术家不仅创造出一个理想客体、映照出他自己自体体验的夸大；而对我们而言更重要的是，艺术家是通过自体体验与艺术客体体验之间的辩证过程来寻求那种完美——二者既融合又分离，在其中，自体体验那些被分裂的诸方面交替地交融与分化。对艺术家来说，坐下来享受自己的劳动果实永远不够；艺术家一次次被迫去重新创造一种自体体验的过程，在其中寻求一种难以捉摸却又有力的情感事件。一旦客体完成，拥有了完全独立的生命，艺术家就必须回到那个过程，回到那持续的寻求。

It is important to note that, although the artist seeks to express self-experience in ideal form through artwork, the idealization must authentically reflect the artist's inner life. Art often depicts forms of idealization that are shallow and inauthentic: pretty pictures that defend against or avoid the articulation of true experience. On the other hand, many great artists seek to portray in the ideal forms of their art the most ugly and grotesque aspects of their fantasy life. In these instances, the creative process involves the creation of a sublime and organized image, which paradoxically articulates the actual, threatened, or imagined fragmentation, distortion, or depletion of the artist's self-experience. This is like the empathic, healing resonance of the analyst, wherein the analyst mirrors the complex and often disturbed inner experience of the patient. The great work of art must be idealized but true, expressing in objective form the depth and breadth of the artist's inner life.

重要的是要指出：尽管艺术家寻求通过艺术作品以理想形式表达自体体验，这种理想化必须真实地反映艺术家的内在生活。艺术常常描绘那些肤浅而虚假的理想化形式：漂亮的画面，用以防御或回避对真实体验的表达。另一方面，许多伟大的艺术家则力求在其艺术的理想形式中，描绘自己幻想生活中最丑陋、最怪诞的方面。在这些情形中，创作过程涉及创造一个崇高而有组织的意象，它悖论性地表达出艺术家自体体验实际存在的、受威胁的或想象中的碎裂、扭曲或耗竭。这有如分析师那种共情的、疗愈性的共鸣——分析师在其中的映照，呈现出病人复杂且常常动荡的内在体验。伟大的艺术作品必须是理想化的、但同时是真实的，它以客观的形式表达出艺术家内在生活的深度与广度。

The source of the creative impulse is the fantasy life of the artist. These fantasies are not simply conscious, but include multiple levels of self-organization, incorporating the most hidden levels of the unconscious. On the other hand, unlike psychological fantasy, the evolving artwork quickly takes on its own nature, which compels the artist to work to express inner fantasy in external forms. Once a mark is put on the canvas or other raw material, inner fantasy changes in response to the external image; this is followed by further action to express fantasy through increasing manipulation of the image. At this point, the distinction between inner processes and outer art object breaks down; it is replaced by a dialectic in which inner and outer processes seek a state of conjunction. Creativity from this perspective is intersubjective, however paradoxically the two subjectivities exist as aspects of the same self-experience. One aspect is concrete and alterable as a medium and a formal structure, while the other is psychical, and both are immersed in a fantastic and imaginative dialogue with each other, out of which experience is increasingly articulated.

创造冲动的来源是艺术家的幻想生活。这些幻想不只是有意识的，而是包含自体组织的多重层次，并囊括了无意识中最隐秘的层面。另一方面，与心理幻想不同，正在演化的作品很快就会呈现出它自己的本性，这迫使艺术家去工作，把内在幻想表达于外在形式之中。一旦在画布或其他原材料上落下一笔，内在幻想就会回应这一外在意象而改变；随后又会有进一步的动作，通过越来越多地对意象的操作来表达幻想。此时，内过程与外在艺术客体之间的区分瓦解了；它被一种辩证关系所取代，在其中内外过程寻求一种契合状态。从这一视角看，创造力是互为主体的[intersubjective]——尽管悖论性地，这两种主体性作为同一个自体体验的不同方面而存在。一个方面是具体的、可作为媒介与形式结构而被改变；另一个方面是心理性的；二者都沉浸于彼此之间一种幻想性、想象性的对话之中，而体验正是从这种对话中被越来越清晰地表达出来。

The creative process comprises three stages, described next. However, creativity is not a linear process, and the stages do not necessarily unfold in a neat progression. In fact, as an artist develops a work, the stages may recycle many times until it is completed. Nonetheless, I believe that the following stages generally fall into the sequence described, and it is heuristically useful to delineate the components of the creative process in this way. By way of introduction, here is a quote from Mark Rothko regarding the earliest phase of a painting:

创作过程包含三个阶段，接下来将逐一描述。然而，创造力并不是一个线性过程，这些阶段也未必以整齐的次序展开。事实上，当艺术家发展一件作品时，这些阶段可能反复循环多次，直到作品完成。尽管如此，我相信下面这些阶段大体上按所描述的次序发生，而如此勾画创作过程的各个组成部分，在启发式上[heuristically]是有用的。作为引子，这里引一段马克·罗斯科[Mark Rothko]关于一幅画最早阶段的话：

It began as an unknown adventure in an unknown space. It is now of completion that in a flash of recognition they are seen to have the quantity and function, which was intended. Ideas and plans that existed in the mind at the start were simply the doorway through which one left the world in which they occurred. (Rothko as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 239)

它始于一片未知空间中的一次未知冒险。如今在完成的时刻，在一闪的辨认之中，它们才被看出具有本所意图的量与功能。起初存在于头脑中的想法与计划，不过是一道门槛，人穿过它，就离开了那些想法与计划所属的世界。 （Rothko，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 239）

The first phase is Inspiration and Self-Crisis. Following upon the desire to create an ideal image of inner life, the artist begins to put brush to canvas. During this phase, the artwork remains fragmentary and incomplete. The artist is in what Kris (1952) referred to as the inspirational phase, in which unconscious fantasy and primary process thinking predominate. Beginning a piece of work is experienced as an opportunity and a risk. This is when the artist works on the primary structure of the piece – that is, the archaic, fragmentary elements that compose the initial phase of the work's development. During this phase, the artist tries to access every aspect of conscious and unconscious subjectivity. Doubt, uncertainty, and confusion may trouble the artist, alternating with joy and exhilaration. Kohut (1976) describes the psychological state of the artist as follows:

第一阶段是 灵感与自体危机 [Inspiration and Self-Crisis]。在创造内在生活之理想意象的愿望之后，艺术家开始执笔落于画布。在这一阶段，作品仍是片段性的、未完成的。艺术家处于克里斯（1952）所称的 灵感 [inspirational] 阶段，在其中，无意识幻想与初级过程思维占主导。开始一件作品，被体验为既是机会也是风险。正是在这时，艺术家处理作品的 初级结构 [primary structure]——即构成作品发展之初始阶段的那些古老的、片段性的元素。在这一阶段，艺术家试图通达有意识与无意识主体性的每一个方面。怀疑、不确定与混乱可能困扰艺术家，并与喜悦和兴奋交替。科胡特（1976）如下描述艺术家的心理状态：

During creative periods the self is at the mercy of powerful forces it cannot control. It feels itself hopelessly exposed to extreme mood swings, which range from severe precreative depression to dangerous hypomanic overstimulation. (p. 818)

在创造期中，自体任凭那些它无法控制的强大力量摆布。它感到自己无望地暴露于极端的情绪波动之中，这些波动从严重的前创造期抑郁，一直到危险的轻躁狂性过度刺激[hypomanic overstimulation]。 （p. 818）

The artist assesses each mark on the canvas and responds with an internal adjustment of fantasy. As Kohut noted, he or she experiences this phase primarily in affective terms; anxiety is followed by pleasure and, hopefully, by self-confidence. In most cases, the experience of crisis occurs on an optimal level. Rather than being derailed or immobilized in the face of an aesthetic challenge, the artist experiences a heightening of attention, a sense of tension and discomfort, which leads to a deepening responsiveness to the work. In some cases, the artist's self-experience is often precarious and at times threatened by the fragmented and ill-defined nature of the artwork. The work during this phase involves the manipulation of the medium and image to sustain and formally articulate a particular self-state.

艺术家评估画布上的每一笔，并以幻想的内在调整作出回应。如科胡特所指出的，他主要是以情感性的方式体验这一阶段；焦虑之后是愉悦，并且希望之后是自信。在多数情况下，危机的体验发生在一个最优的水平上。面对审美上的挑战，艺术家不是被带离轨道或被冻结僵住，而是体验到注意力的提升、一种紧张与不适之感，这导向对作品更深的回应性。在某些情形中，艺术家的自体体验常常是不稳固的，并且有时受到作品那种片段性与界定不清之性质的威胁。这一阶段的工作，涉及对媒介与意象的操作，以维持并从形式上表达某一特定的自体状态。

A picture is not thought out and settled beforehand. While it is being done it changes as one's thoughts change. And when it is finished, it still goes on changing, according to the state of mind of whoever is looking at it. A picture lives a life like a living creature, undergoing the changes imposed on us by our life from day to day. This is natural enough, as the picture lives only through the man who is looking at it. (Picasso as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 202)

一幅画不是事先想好、事先定下的。在它被画出来的过程中，它随人的想法改变而改变。而当它完成之后，它依然在变，取决于看它的人的心境。一幅画像活物一样过着自己的一生，承受着我们日复一日的生活所施加于它的变化。这十分自然，因为这幅画只通过看它的人而活着。 （Picasso，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 202）

This quotation captures the dialectic between the inner subjectivity of the artist and the external subjectivity of the artwork. Both seem to have a life of their own, but they are also profoundly and ineluctably linked. The artwork is like a growing and changing, living thing, but it lives only through the person who looks at it. Moreover, the observer is also altered by the work. At a certain point in the creative process, the artist begins to experience a state of aesthetic resonance with his or her creation.

这段引言捕捉到了艺术家的内在主体性与作品的外在主体性之间的辩证关系。二者似乎都各有自己的生命，但它们又深刻地、无可逃避地联系在一起。艺术作品像一个生长变化着的活物，但它只通过看它的人而活着。此外，观看者也被作品所改变。在创作过程的某一时点，艺术家开始体验到一种与自己创造物之间的审美共鸣状态。

The second phase is aesthetic resonance. Once the artist begins to experience a growing conjunction of feeling and image, this phase can begin. Here the feeling that the artwork is beginning to take form excites the artist. There is an increased experience of self-cohesion, effectiveness, and vitality. The artist feels in tune with the artwork, and there is a sense of resonance in which the piece seems to become a perfect reflection of the artist's inner fantasy life. I use the term aesthetic resonance in this context to describe the intensification of feeling that the artist experiences, both internally and externally, due to the conjunction between self and work. This is the most affectively charged moment of the creative experience, in which the artist feels the presence of the sublime and feels confirmed in his or her grandiosity.

第二阶段是 审美共鸣 [aesthetic resonance]。一旦艺术家开始体验到感受与意象之间日益增长的契合，这一阶段就可以开始。在此，作品开始成形这一感受令艺术家兴奋。自体凝聚、有效性与活力的体验增强。艺术家感到与作品合调，并且有一种共鸣感——作品似乎成了艺术家内在幻想生活的完美映照。我在此语境中使用 审美共鸣 一词，来描述艺术家因自体与作品之间的契合而在内部与外部所体验到的那种感受的强化。这是创造体验中情感负荷最强的时刻，艺术家在其中感受到崇高的临在，并感到自己的夸大得到了确认。

Jackson Pollock described how he unrolled his canvas onto the floor and used unconventional tools to experience himself as literally "in" the painting.

杰克逊·波洛克[Jackson Pollock]描述了他如何把画布摊开在地板上，并使用非传统的工具，让自己体验为字面意义上"身处"画作之中。

When I am in my painting, I'm not aware of what I am doing. It is only after a sort of "getting acquainted" period that I see what I have been about. I have no fears about making changes, destroying the image, etc., because the painting has a life of its own. I try to let it come through. It is only when I lose contact with the painting that the result is a mess. Otherwise, there is pure harmony, an easy give and take, and the painting comes out well. (Pollock as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 253)

当我身处我的画中时，我并不知道自己在做什么。只有在经历了一段"熟悉彼此"的时期之后，我才看清自己一直在做什么。我不害怕做出改动、毁掉意象之类的事，因为画有自己的生命。我试图让它透出来。只有当我与画失去接触时，结果才是一片混乱。否则，那里只有纯粹的和谐、一种轻松的给予与取得，画就画得很好。 （Pollock，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 253）

In artistic creation, the object is never experienced as fully a part of the self, and the continuing recognition of the unique qualities of the artwork is essential. In other words, during the process of creating, the experience of merger with the artwork is necessary, but this means more than the object experienced as part of the self, equally as much, it means that the self is experienced as merged with the object. The artist feels that he or she is the artwork, and in fact, this is true; it is not an illusion. For an artwork to be truly a new creation, it must be structured by the artist's self-experience. It must exist both out in the world and psychologically within the artist.

在艺术创造中，客体从未被完全体验为自体的一部分，而持续地辨认出作品那些独特的品质至关重要。换言之，在创造的过程中，与作品融合的体验是必要的；但这不仅意味着客体被体验为自体的一部分，同样也意味着自体被体验为与客体融合。艺术家感到自己就是这件作品——而事实上，这是真的，它不是幻觉。一件艺术作品若要真正成为新的创造，它就必须由艺术家的自体体验所结构。它必须既存在于外在世界，也存在于艺术家的心理内部。

However, the artist must remain aware of the reality and physical nature of the evolving artwork. In fact, his or her self-experience is powerfully linked to this other, which is both a part of the artist and profoundly and significantly separate. If this differentiation were to collapse, if a full experience of merger occurred and persisted, the creative process would cease, and the artist would be relegated to the status of a common dreamer.

然而，艺术家必须持续觉察到正在演化的作品之现实性与物理性质。事实上，他的自体体验与这个他者强有力地相连——这个他者既是艺术家的一部分，又深刻地、显著地与他分离。如果这种分化崩溃了，如果一种完全的融合体验发生并持续下去，创作过程就会停止，艺术家也会被降格为一个寻常的白日梦者。

From the first moment of creation, the artist engages in a dialectic with the selfobject/artwork; this dialectic is the engine of creativity. The artist extends his or her subjectivity outward and alters the medium. From this point on, something new exists, which has been created out of a merger between the self-experience of the artist and the world. The work in progress comes into being. The artist observes the work, appraises it, and a dialectic ensues between that which has been created and the subjectivity and judgment of the artist. The important thing is that the dialectic occurs between two realms of self-experience: the internal/subjective and the external/objective. Each influences the other (the imaginary and the manifest), and the result is further creation.

从创造的第一刻起，艺术家就与自体客体/作品进行着一种辩证互动；这种辩证互动是创造力的引擎。艺术家把自己的主体性向外延伸，并改变媒介。从这一刻起，某种新的东西存在着——它诞生于艺术家的自体体验与世界之间的一种融合。进行中的作品开始形成。艺术家观察作品、评估作品，于是在已被创造之物与艺术家的主体性及判断之间，一种辩证互动随之展开。要紧的是，这种辩证互动发生在自体体验的两个领域之间：内部/主体的，以及外部/客观的。二者相互影响（想象之物与显明之物），其结果就是进一步的创造。

Thus, the artwork is increasingly elaborated toward a level of refinement. In other words, the artist finds in creativity an opportunity to give form to subjective experience in an increasingly articulate and formally refined manner. There is output, feedback, response/output, dialectic within the realm of the artist's subjectivity. The artist's measure and guide for this process is the delicate sense of resonance that he or she experiences with the developing artwork. Mondrian (1937) uses the term intensification to describe this process, in which the artist creates "successively profound planes." Through intensification, rather than simply repeating cultural or natural forms, the artist elaborates something truly new and emotionally stirring.

于是，作品被越来越精细地加工，趋向某种精致的水准。换言之，艺术家在创造力中找到一个机会，以越来越清晰、在形式上越来越精致的方式，赋予主观体验以形式。这里有输出、反馈、回应/输出，以及艺术家主体性领域之内的辩证互动。艺术家对这一过程的衡量与引导，是他与正在发展的作品之间所体验到的那种精微的共鸣感。蒙德里安[Mondrian]（1937）使用 强化 [intensification] 一词来描述这一过程，在其中艺术家创造出"层层深入的平面"["successively profound planes"]。通过强化，艺术家不是在单纯重复文化或自然的形式，而是在精心制作某种真正新的、情感上动人[emotionally stirring]的东西。

The most important tool the artist fashions through constant practice is faith in his ability to produce miracles when they are needed. (Rothko as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 239)

艺术家通过持续不断的练习所打造的、最重要的工具，是一种信念：相信自己有能力在需要时创造奇迹。 （Rothko，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 239）

The experience of aesthetic resonance, while essential to the elaborative phase of the creativity previously discussed, is not the only important factor in successful creativity. Inevitably, there is an experience of failure in resonance, of selfobject failure. Kohut (1985) put it this way: "Creative people tend to alternate during periods of productivity between phases when they think extremely highly of their work and phases when they are convinced that it has no value" (p. 114). It is the artist's response to this sense of failure that further intensifies the creative effort, compelling him or her to elaborate ever more radical formal organizations. In most cases, this stage begins when the artist's feeling of certainty gives way to sober reflection and reassessment. The artist may come to doubt the work's perfection, and it no longer seems to perfectly reflect the feelings that the artist wishes to capture. (Ehrenzweig [1967a] describes this experience as the "grayness of the morning after" when the artist must confront the flawed reality of what was felt to be sublime just the day before.)

审美共鸣的体验，虽然对先前所讨论的创造力精加工阶段至关重要，却并不是成功创造力的唯一重要因素。不可避免地，会出现共鸣的失败、即自体客体失败的体验。科胡特（1985）这样说："有创造力的人在多产时期往往在两种阶段之间交替：一种阶段他们认为自己的作品极其出色，另一种阶段他们确信它毫无价值"（p. 114）。正是艺术家对这种失败感的回应，进一步强化了创造性的努力，迫使他去精心制作越来越激进的形式组织。在多数情况下，这一阶段始于艺术家的确定感让位于冷静的反思与重新评估。艺术家可能开始怀疑作品的完美，它似乎不再完美地反映艺术家希望捕捉的那些感受。（艾伦茨威格[1967a]把这一体验描述为"次日清晨的灰暗"，此时艺术家必须面对前一天还被感受为崇高之物那有缺陷的现实。）

This phase is characterized by a sense of failure, in which the resonance breaks down and the artist must use all his or her skill and resourcefulness to reassess the image and rework it to restore the aesthetic tie. The selfobject experience is restored when the work is once again felt to capture the ideal and grandiose components of fantasy. As in the first phase, the experience of selfobject failure during this phase should remain within certain limits of intensity. As with the optimal frustrations described by Kohut, the selfobject failure experienced at this point in the creative process should not be traumatic in most cases. There may be some heightened anxiety and even depression, but as with optimal frustration, the artist should be able to rally and address the aesthetic problems that he or she is faced with. The artist attempts to restore the selfobject tie not through the accrual of self-structure, but through alteration of the artwork.

这一阶段的特征是一种失败感：共鸣崩塌了，艺术家必须动用他全部的技能与机巧，重新评估意象、重做作品，以恢复那审美的联结。当作品再次被感受为捕捉到了幻想中那些理想性的与夸大的成分时，自体客体体验就得到了修复。与第一阶段一样，这一阶段中自体客体失败的体验应保持在一定的强度限度之内。如同科胡特所描述的最优挫折[optimal frustrations]，在创作过程的这一点上所体验到的自体客体失败，在多数情况下不应是创伤性的。可能会有某种加剧的焦虑甚至抑郁，但如同最优挫折的情形，艺术家应当能够振作起来，处理他所面临的审美问题。艺术家试图修复自体客体联结的方式，不是通过自体结构的积累[self-structure]，而是通过改变作品。

During this phase, preconscious and conscious processes play a part in organizing and clarifying the elements in the artwork, as the artist attempts to bring it into harmony with self-experience. I call this activity working on the secondary structure of the artwork, in that, rather than relying on inspiration and primary process thinking, the artist approaches the work more synthetically, with an eye toward the total organization of the work and the relationships among its formal components. This is like Kris's (1952) notion of the elaboration phase. However, while Kris stressed the use of secondary process thinking during this period, I believe that the artist must continue to respond to the work on multiple psychic levels. Like what occurs in the case of transmuting internalization, the response to a break in the selfobject tie is the accrual of structure; here the artist refines and strengthens the artwork's formal organization to make it more resonant with fantasy. At the same time, the artist's inner experience changes in response to the artwork as well. The result is the restoration of the selfobject tie between artist and artwork.

在这一阶段，前意识与有意识的过程参与对作品中各要素的组织与澄清，因为艺术家试图把作品带进与自体体验的和谐之中。我把这一活动称为 处理作品的次级结构 [working on the secondary structure]：在此，艺术家不是依靠灵感与初级过程思维，而是以更综合性的方式接近作品，着眼于作品的整体组织及其诸形式成分之间的关系。这类似于克里斯（1952）关于精加工阶段的观念。然而，尽管克里斯强调在这一时期使用次级过程思维，我相信艺术家必须继续在多个心理层面上回应作品。如同在 转变性内化 [transmuting internalization] 的情形中所发生的，对自体客体联结之断裂的回应是结构的累积；在此，艺术家提纯并强化作品的形式组织，使其与幻想更加共鸣。与此同时，艺术家的内在体验也回应作品而改变。其结果是艺术家与作品之间自体客体联结的修复。

When you begin a picture, you often make some pretty discoveries. You must be on your guard against these. Destroy the thing, do it over several times. In each distorting of a beautiful discovery, the artist does not really suppress it, but rather transforms it, condenses it, makes it more substantial. What comes in the end is the result of discarded finds. Otherwise, you become your own connoisseur. I sell myself nothing. (Picasso as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 203)

当你开始画一幅画时，常常会有一些漂亮的发现。你必须对这些发现保持戒备。毁掉它，重来几次。在每一次对某个美丽发现的变形中，艺术家并非真的压制它，而是转化它、凝缩它，使它更为充实。最终呈现出来的东西，是被丢弃的发现之结果。否则，你就成了自己的鉴赏家。我什么也不卖给自己。 （Picasso，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 203）

Kohut (1966) compared creativity to addiction, and in a sense, this is apt. I would, however, modify this comparison somewhat by saying that the artist's addiction is to the process of selfobject failure and restoration. The artist is driven to seek out again and again the experience of heightened, lost, and regained self-experience. Central to the addictive nature of this process is the artist's experience of doing it him- or herself, this may be what Rank (1932) referred to as the artist's quest for self-begetting and self-rebirth. As Stolorow and Atwood (1993) stated in their analysis of Rank: "The artist's own self-created differentiated self is his first creative work, and throughout his life remains fundamentally his chief work" (p. 45). (Rank argued that the artist seeks to transcend reality and mortality.)

科胡特（1966）把创造力比作成瘾，在某种意义上，这一类比是恰当的。不过，我要对这一类比稍作修改：艺术家的成瘾，是对自体客体失败与修复这一过程的成瘾。艺术家被驱使着一次次去寻求那种自体体验被提升、丧失又重获的经验。这一过程的成瘾性质之核心，是艺术家"由自己来做"的经验——这或许就是兰克[Rank]（1932）所称的艺术家对自我生育[self-begetting]与自我重生[self-rebirth]的追寻。正如斯托罗洛与阿特伍德[Stolorow and Atwood]（1993）在对兰克的分析中所说的："艺术家自己创造出来的、已分化的自体，是他的第一件创造性作品，并且终其一生，从根本上始终是他最重要的作品"（p. 45）。（兰克主张，艺术家寻求超越现实与必死性。）

Albert Pinkham Ryder expressed this motivation for his work:

阿尔伯特·平克姆·赖德[Albert Pinkham Ryder]表达了他从事创作的这一动机：

When I stood before my easel with its square of stretched canvas, I realized that I had in my possession the wherewith to create a masterpiece that would live throughout the coming ages. I at once proceeded to study the works of the great to discover how best to achieve immortality with a square of canvas and a box of colors. (Ryder as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 150)

当我站在画架前，面对那一方绷紧的画布，我意识到自己手中握有创造一件杰作的条件——那杰作将在此后诸多世代中长存。我随即着手研究那些伟大的作品，去发现如何最好地以一方画布和一盒颜料达成不朽。 （Ryder，转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 150）

I would argue that the artist repeatedly seeks to transcend the experience of selfobject failure and that, by means of creative effort, the artist experiences his or her own power in bringing about the restoration of self-experience. In most cases, the aesthetic resonance with the completed artwork will fade, and although the work may always hold some positive meaning for the artist, the search for transcendence and renewal must be re-engaged.

我要主张，艺术家一次次寻求超越自体客体失败的体验；并且，凭借创造性的努力，艺术家体验到自己在带来自体体验之修复上的力量。在多数情况下，与已完成作品之间的审美共鸣会消退；尽管作品对艺术家而言可能始终具有某种正向的意义，但那对超越与更新的追寻必须被重新投入。

It is this complex dialectic between artist and artwork that drives the artist to seek increasingly refined and sophisticated, formal means to express subjectivity. The artist who engages in this process is driven to repeatedly seek out the wonder of the aesthetic selfobject experience, therefore, he or she must continually work to articulate and perfect a formally organized and ideal representation of lived experience. The skillful and talented artist can feel the potential for this experience in artistic activity; and this is perhaps the source of the feeling of joy that Kligerman described in an earlier quotation. In a sense, the successful artist is possessed, even driven, to seek restoration of this experience, until he or she reaches a point at which there is a conjunction between inner experience and outer image.

正是艺术家与作品之间这一复杂的辩证互动，驱使艺术家去寻找越来越精炼、越来越精致的形式手段来表达主体性。投入这一过程的艺术家，被驱使着一次次去寻求审美自体客体体验的奇妙；因此，他必须持续地工作，以表达并完善对活生生经验的一种在形式上有机组织起来的、理想的表现。技巧娴熟而富有才华的艺术家，能在艺术活动中感受到这一体验的潜能；这或许就是克利格曼在前引文中所描述的那种喜悦感受的来源。在某种意义上，成功的艺术家被占有、甚至被驱使着去寻求这一体验的修复，直到他达到一个内在体验与外在意象相契合的点。

Matisse stated this quite simply when he was asked how he knew that a piece was finished:

当有人问马蒂斯[Matisse]他如何知道一件作品完成了时，他说得很简单：

[I know] when it expresses my emotion as completely as possible. It is at that point when the sense of resonance is enduring and durable. The artist assessing the work does not experience anxiety, depression or fragmentation, but sureness and calm. The artist no longer feels compelled to change the work because it feels balanced and complete. The work is done. It exists as a thing. (Matisse as quoted in Barr, 1951, p. 286)

[我知道]当它尽可能完整地表达我的情绪时。正是在那一刻，共鸣感是持久而耐久的。评估作品的艺术家所体验到的不是焦虑、抑郁或碎裂，而是笃定与平静。艺术家不再感到被迫去改变作品，因为它感觉平衡而完整。作品完成了。它作为一个物[thing]而存在。 （Matisse，转引自 Barr, 1951, p. 286）

And Mark Rothko described the artist's relationship to the completed work:

而马克·罗斯科这样描述艺术家与已完成作品的关系：

Pictures must be miraculous: the instant one is completed, the intimacy between the creation and the creator is ended. He is an outsider. The picture must be for him, as for anyone experiencing it later, a revelation, an unexpected and unprecedented resolution of an eternally familiar need. (as quoted in Proter, 1997, p. 239)

画必须是奇迹般的：一件作品一完成，创造物与创造者之间的亲密就结束了。他成了一个局外人。这幅画对他而言——就像对后来任何体验它的人而言一样——必须是一种启示，是对一种永恒熟悉之需要的意外而未之前有的解决。 （转引自 Proter, 1997, p. 239）

In summary, I have in this section made the following points about the creative process:

总而言之，我在本节中就创作过程提出了以下几点：

• Art is the externalization of self-experience. It is what Langer calls objectification of subjectivity, or, more simply, the symbolization of feeling. • The motivation to create is characterized by the desire for idealization of self-experience. • As a result, during the creative process, the artist engages in a dialectic with his or her own idealized subjectivity in the objectified form of the developing artwork. • The feeling of harmony (aesthetic resonance) that the artist experiences with the work in progress is a form of selfobject experience. The artist longs to establish this form of selfobject tie. • However, while creating a particular artwork, the artist inevitably experiences the selfobject tie as elusive, precarious, or broken. The artist is repeatedly disillusioned as to the work's perfection. • The artist seeks to strengthen or restore the selfobject tie through its gradual refinement. By this means, the artwork is moved closer to a formal ideal. • The repeated experience of self-restoration and creation (and re-creation) of a truly ideal object out of oneself explains the addictionlike quality of creativity. Through creation, the artist triumphs over the ineluctability of selfobject failure and the vulnerability of self-experience. • In the end, the artist's fantasy is that he or she will surmount personal mortality through art.

• 艺术是自体体验的外化。它就是朗格所称的 主体性的客体化 [objectification of subjectivity]，或者更简单地说，是 感受 的象征化。 • 创造的动机，其特点是对自体体验加以理想化的愿望。 • 因此，在创作过程中，艺术家以正在发展的作品这一被客体化的形式，与他自身被理想化的主体性进行辩证互动。 • 艺术家在与进行中的作品相处时所体验到的和谐感（审美共鸣），是一种自体客体体验。艺术家渴望建立这种形式的自体客体联结。 • 然而，在创作某一具体作品时，艺术家不可避免地会把自体客体联结体验为难以捉摸的、不稳固的、或者已断裂的。艺术家一次次对作品的完美感到幻灭。 • 艺术家通过逐步的精炼，寻求加强或修复自体客体联结。借此，作品被带得离某个形式理想更近。 • 从自身之中反复经验到自我修复，以及一个真正理想客体的创造（与再创造），解释了创造力那如成瘾一般的性质。通过创造，艺术家战胜了自体客体失败的无可逃避，也战胜了自体体验的脆弱。 • 最终，艺术家的幻想是：他将通过艺术超越个人的必死性。

In the next section, I will present the treatment of a young artist and discuss some of the typical disturbances that can occur in the creative process. This case was chosen for the way in which the patient experienced transitory problems during each phase of creativity. Childhood sources of his self-vulnerability will be touched upon.

在下一节中，我将呈现一位年轻艺术家的治疗，并讨论创作过程中可能出现的一些典型困扰。选择这个案例，是因为病人在创造力的每一阶段都体验到暂时性的问题。对他自体脆弱性[self-vulnerability]的童年来源也会有所触及。

Clinical Illustration（临床例证）

Several years ago, I saw a young painter, Rob, in psychoanalytic psychotherapy. He had been working as an artist for some time and was on the verge of receiving much-deserved recognition. Initially, he had come to treatment after the breakup of a six-year relationship with a woman upon whom he was extremely dependent. He continued to be despondent for several months, but his motivation to paint remained high. In fact, he seemed to use his artwork to express his despair and loneliness. For a time, his work became dominated by varieties of grays and dark earth tones.

几年前，我在精神分析心理治疗中会见了一位年轻的画家，罗布[Rob]。他作为艺术家工作了一段时间，并且正处于即将获得他理应得到的认可的边缘。最初，他是在与一位他极为依赖的女性六年关系破裂之后前来治疗的。他在数月间持续消沉，但他作画的动力依然很高。事实上，他似乎是用自己的作品来表达绝望与孤独。有一段时间，他的作品被各种各样的灰色与暗沉的大地色调所主导。

About six months into the treatment, Rob received an invitation to hold a one-man show at a downtown gallery. Although he had exhibited in group shows frequently, this was his first solo exhibition. He was elated. Having viewed himself as a struggling junior artist for some years, he saw this as his chance to make it big. He worked at preparing the show for several weeks, day and night, and when the show opened, the reviews were generally positive, even laudatory in some cases. However, Rob was almost literally paralyzed by self-doubt.

治疗进行到大约六个月时，罗布收到邀请，要在一家市中心的画廊举办个展。尽管他经常参加群展，这是他第一次个人展览。他欣喜若狂。多年来他一直把自己看作一个苦苦挣扎的年轻艺术家，他视此为出人头地的机会。他日夜工作，花了几周时间准备展览；展览开幕时，评论总体上是正面的，在某些情形下甚至充满赞誉。然而，罗布几乎可以说是被自我怀疑[self-doubt]瘫痪了。

It is not uncommon for artists to experience this type of response to success. The state of vulnerability experienced during exhibitions, especially early in the artist's career, can lead to difficult-to-manage excitement and profound disappointment. However, for the purpose of this chapter, what interested me about Rob's reaction was the subsequent impact of his success on his creative process. In general, until this point, he had been free of work inhibitions or other conflicts around creativity.

艺术家对成功产生这类反应并不罕见。在展览期间所体验到的脆弱状态，尤其是在艺术家生涯的早期，可能导致难以驾驭的兴奋与深切的失望。然而，就本章的目的而言，罗布的反应中令我感兴趣的是他的成功随后对创作过程造成的影响。总的来说，直到这一刻之前，他都没有工作上的抑制[work inhibitions]或围绕创造力的其他冲突。

He had produced a large amount of work with a reasonable amount of effort; but several weeks after his solo show, when he had begun to work on a series of dramatic and very ambitious pastels, he started to come to his psychotherapy sessions in a state of panic.

他以相当合理的努力制作了大量作品；但在个展几周之后，当他开始创作一系列戏剧性的、极具雄心的粉彩画时，他开始以一种惊恐的状态前来参加心理治疗会谈。

"I can't seem to get a handle on things," Rob complained. "I know basically what I want to do. I can almost taste it. I feel like I'm on the verge of something different, but it's all just a mess. I can't seem to pull it together. I just sit and stare at these blotches of color. It's as if all the talent is gone, just gone. I don't know what to do."

"我似乎没办法把一切理顺，"罗布抱怨道。"我大致知道自己想做什么。我几乎能尝到它的味道。我觉得自己正处于某种不同之物的边缘，但一切只是一团乱。我似乎没法把它聚拢起来。我就只是坐着，盯着这些色块。就好像所有的才华都消失了，就这样消失了。我不知道该怎么办。"

As we talked, it became clear that Rob's expectations of himself had become extremely high, and his need to prove his talent led to anxiety as he struggled with the early inchoate phase of his new productions. "Before, I used to be able to just go with it," he explained. "I'd sit back and say to myself, stay calm, just wait a bit and it will come together. But now, it's like the stakes are so high; I feel like so much is expected of me. I'm no longer just a struggling artist, but also feel that I haven't really made it. It could all fall apart so easily."

随着我们交谈，事情变得清楚：罗布对自己的期望已变得极高，而他证明自己才华的需要导致焦虑，因为他正艰难应对新作品那早期尚未成形的阶段。"以前，我能够顺其自然，"他解释说。"我会放松下来对自己说，保持冷静，稍等一会儿，它就会聚拢起来。但现在，就像赌注太高了；我感到大家对我寄予了太多期待。我不再只是一个苦苦挣扎的艺术家，但同时也觉得自己其实并没有真正成功。这一切可能很容易就瓦解。"

In fact, Rob had begun to feel that his career had fallen apart. As he struggled with each new drawing, he was unable to sustain his self-confidence or his focus during the confusing early phases of his creative process. His need for a sense of confirmation, which resulted from his sense of vulnerability to selfobject failure, transformed the challenge of each new drawing into evidence of his fundamental, and now undeniable, failure.

事实上，罗布已开始感到自己的事业分崩离析。当他艰难应对每一幅新画时，在创作过程那些令人困惑的早期阶段，他无法维持自信或专注。他对一种确认感的需要——源自他对自体客体失败的脆弱感——把每一幅新画带来的挑战，都变成他根本性的、如今无可否认的失败的证据。

Rob was gradually able to recover his self-confidence, but his sense of vulnerability and precarious self-cohesion remained a problem throughout all phases of his work. For example, when he was finally able to move beyond the early phase of a work in progress and had developed a sense of rhythm and renewed excitement, he began to wake up in the morning in a state of dread. A particular piece of work, one that had so inspired him the day before, would now appear to be a "train wreck." That image perfectly captured his self-experience: "It's like I'm just cruising along, and everything is coming together, and then the first bump in the road, wham, out of control, and then crash! I feel like it's all gone and it's never coming back again."

罗布逐渐能够恢复自信，但他那种脆弱感以及不稳固的自体凝聚，在他工作的各个阶段始终是个问题。例如，当他终于能够走出进行中作品的那个早期阶段，并发展出一种节律感和重新燃起的兴奋时，他开始在早晨带着恐惧醒来。某一件作品——前一天还如此激励他——如今看来却是一场"车祸现场"["train wreck"]。这个意象完美地捕捉了他的自体体验："就像我正一路顺畅地行驶，一切都在聚拢起来，然后路上第一个颠簸，砰，失控了，然后撞车！我觉得一切都没了，而且再也不会回来了。"

Most artists, even when a work seems to be falling into place, reach a point where the sense of harmony and perfect fit is disrupted. The challenge is then to recover and solve the problem through a reassessment, and oftentimes a reworking, of the piece. The capacity to sustain enough self-experience to repair the sense of disruption is crucial. Even the worst "train wreck" may be a necessary stage leading to creative advancement. Rob's sense of self was so tenuous that the selfobject failure typical of this phase resulted in collapse and a sense of fragmentation.

大多数艺术家，即便在一件作品看来正逐步成形时，也会到达一个点：和谐感与完美契合感被打破了。接下来的挑战是通过重新评估、且往往是重做这件作品，来恢复并解决问题。维持足够的自体体验以修复那种断裂感的能力至关重要。即便最糟糕的"车祸现场"，也可能是通往创造性进展的必经阶段。罗布的自体感如此脆弱，以致这一阶段典型的自体客体失败导致了崩塌与碎裂感。

A crucial part of Rob's recovery from artistic success was his experience of me as both consistent and confident in him. He saw me as "having my feet on the ground," and explained that when he was in session, he was able to "retool" himself and get a handle on his work. Eventually, he was able to struggle through and create some interesting work, but even then, he remained afraid to show completed pieces even to close friends. It was as if he saw every flaw and imperfection. He felt ashamed of his self-doubts. He remembered that when he was a child, his father had criticized him for acting like a "big shot."

罗布从艺术成功中恢复过来的一个关键部分，是他体验到我一贯如一、并且对他有信心。他把我视为"脚踏实地"的人，并解释说在会谈中，他能够"重整装备"["retool"]自己，重新把握住自己的工作。最终，他能够挣扎着走出来，创作出一些有意思的作品；但即便在那个时候，他仍然害怕把完成的作品展示给哪怕亲近的朋友。就好像他能看到每一处瑕疵与不完美。他为自己的自我怀疑感到羞耻。他记得自己小时候，父亲曾批评他表现得像个"大人物"["big shot"]。

"That's it, that's it!" Rob seemed almost elated.

"就是这样，就是这样！"罗布几乎显得兴高采烈。

I feel like I've been paying the price for being a big shot. I never felt with my dad that it was all right to be a big shot. I don't even think I was, but he would always criticize me as if I was doing something wrong. The show that was my undoing. I was acting like a big shot when I am just a kid trying to please my dad. But I never could. I don't remember any time when I really felt like I did anything good. One time, when I was in high school, I had just won a big race, and I was all excited. He comes up to me and all he says (in front of my friends) is, "Put your sweater on." I was pissed off. Suddenly, my excitement was gone – all I felt was anger.

我觉得自己一直在为大人物这个身份付出代价。和我爸在一起，我从来不觉得当个大人物是没问题的。我甚至不觉得自己真的是，但他总是批评我，好像我做错了什么。那个展览就是使我崩溃的原因。我只是一个想讨爸爸欢心的孩子，却在表现得像个大人物。可我从来没能做到。我不记得有哪一次我真的觉得自己做成了什么好事。有一次，我上高中，我刚赢了一场大赛跑，特别兴奋。他走过来，只说了一句（当着我朋友的面）："把毛衣穿上。"我气坏了。突然之间，我的兴奋就不见了——我感到的只有愤怒。

Rob's long-anticipated success was followed by a period of self-crisis, during which his experience of shame and selfobject failure impacted on every phase of his creative work. The precariousness of his self-experience resulted in an increased need for mirroring, as well as his craving for the presence of an idealized, reassuring figure. As a result, he was unable to sustain confidence in the face of routine experiences of doubt and confusion that had always been a part of his creative process.

罗布期盼已久的成功之后，是一段自体危机[self-crisis]时期；在此期间，他羞耻与自体客体失败的体验影响了其创作工作的每一个阶段。他那不稳固的自体体验导致了对镜像的更大需要，以及对一个被理想化的、令人安心的形象之在场的渴望。结果，他无法在面对那些一直属于创作过程一部分的、习常的怀疑与困惑体验时维持自信。

Over time, Rob struggled with each instance of uncertainty as if it represented his ultimate unworthiness. His fragile capacity to sustain or recover self-experience led to extreme states of self-crisis, rather than to a sense of challenge and opportunity. Gradually, through his growing awareness of the sources of his vulnerability in his relationship with his father, and by virtue of his experience of my availability and confidence in him, he was able to come to terms with the meaning of his success and to move on with his career.

随着时间推移，罗布把每一处不确定都当作自己根本无价值的证据来苦苦挣扎。他维持或恢复自体体验的能力脆弱，这导致了极端的自体危机状态，而不是一种挑战与机会之感。渐渐地，通过对自己脆弱性在其与父亲关系中的来源日益增长的觉察，也凭借他对我之可及性[availability]与对他之信心的体验，他得以与成功的意义达成和解，并在事业上继续前行。

Here I would like to add a few words regarding the creative artist's challenge to conventions – something that is frequently seen, at least in modern Western art. It would seem from a cursory look at the lives of many modern artists that they exhibit what appears to be a driving need to challenge and provoke. Creativity has become synonymous with the different and the new, and the image of the artist as an outsider and rebel has become a common cultural stereotype. At least superficially, artists are more interested in disruption and conflict than in cohesion, continuity, and vitality.

在此我想就创造性艺术家对成规[conventions]的挑战再说几句——这一现象至少在现代西方艺术中屡见不鲜。粗略地看一眼许多现代艺术家的生平，似乎会让人觉得，他们表现出一种驱迫性的挑战与挑衅需要。创造力已成了"不同"与"新"的同义词，而艺术家作为局外人和反叛者的形象，已成为一种常见的文化刻板印象。至少从表面上看，艺术家对断裂与冲突的兴趣，大于对凝聚、连续与活力的兴趣。

If this view of the artist is valid, how can it be reconciled with the notion of the artist's search for selfobject experience? Rotenberg (1992) addressed this question in claiming that the creative artist is fundamentally driven by a desire to challenge conventional forms of thinking. He developed a set of terms for this trait – optimal, operative, and perversity – with the following definitions:

如果这种关于艺术家的看法是成立的，那么它如何与"艺术家寻求自体客体体验"这一观念相协调？罗滕伯格（1992）回应了这个问题，他主张创造性的艺术家根本上是被一种挑战惯例性思维方式的愿望所驱动。他为这一特质发展出一组术语——最优的 [optimal]、操作的 [operative] 与 悖常 [perversity]——并给出如下定义：

Perversity is the expression of the artist who consistently, and with technical means, contradicts a previously held principle of organization [p. 171]. . . . The introduction of elements that are original or new results in perception whose implications have not yet been assimilated into the order-making structures of the self [p. 172]. . . . Optimal refers to the quality and degree of unusualness that also achieves integration with the rest of the pictorial field of which it is a part [p. 177] . . . Operative refers to the enacted quality of the artwork and to the technical skill that makes it possible. [p. 179]

Rotenberg believed that artists are driven to produce work with an unusual organization of meaning and form, which also achieves new levels of aesthetic integration. Once again, I believe that the desire to challenge convention is principally based on the artist's desire to truly express self-experience. The ability of conventional or traditional forms of art to express the subjective experience of artists over time is limited. The artist seeks to challenge conventions in the process of discovering new, more powerful means to express self-experience. It would be useless for an artist to adopt another artist's style unless he or she believed that use of that style or technique could convey his or her own experience. In addition, in recalling the artist's search for selfobject experience, we might view the revolutionary artist's underlying desire as the wish to be responded to as a unique and special person.

罗滕伯格相信，艺术家被驱动着去创作出在意义与形式上具有非常组织方式、同时也达成新的审美整合水平的作品。我再次认为，挑战成规的愿望主要基于艺术家想要真正表达自体体验的愿望。随时间推移，惯例性或传统性的艺术形式表达艺术家主观体验的能力是有限的。艺术家在发现新的、更有力的表达自体体验之手段的过程中，寻求挑战成规。艺术家采用另一位艺术家的风格将毫无用处，除非他相信使用那种风格或技法能够传达他自己的经验。此外，在回顾艺术家对自体客体体验的寻求时，我们可以把这位革命性艺术家的深层愿望视为：希望被作为一个独特而特别的人来回应。

The bottom line is that the challenge of modern art is the desire to more completely, accurately, and vividly convey the experience of living in the modern world. Jackson Pollock said that new times require new art forms, and this is true; but in addition, new people who require new forms of self-expression populate new times. The old forms rarely suffice, unless the artist can find new potentials or new facets in them that can be made relevant to current experience.

归根结底，现代艺术的挑战，是想要更完整、更准确、更生动地传达在现代世界中活着的体验。杰克逊·波洛克说，新的时代需要新的艺术形式，这话是对的；但除此之外，新的时代里住着需要新自我表达形式的新人。旧形式很少够用，除非艺术家能在其中找到可以使之与当下体验相关的新的潜能或新的侧面。

For example, one of the most influential revolutionary movements in the history of modern art was abstract expressionism. These artists were viewed by many of the time as wild radicals seeking only to undermine and damage artistic traditions. Jackson Pollock, a prominent abstract expressionist, was once referred to in print as "Jack the Dripper," due to his use of dripped or poured paint. It is certainly true that these artists sought to create a new, radical vision of art. However, when one looks closely at their statements and writings, it appears that their primary motive was not only to disrupt, but also to create new ways of expressing their self-experiences. Not content with conflict, virtually all of them longed for professional and social recognition of their work.

例如，现代艺术史上最具影响力的革命性运动之一，就是抽象表现主义[abstract expressionism]。当时的许多人把这些艺术家视为只想破坏和损害艺术传统的狂野激进分子。杰克逊·波洛克——一位杰出的抽象表现主义者——曾因其使用滴洒或倾倒颜料的手法，在出版物中被称作"滴洒的杰克"["Jack the Dripper"]。这些艺术家确实曾寻求创造一种新的、激进的艺术观。然而，当人仔细审视他们的言论与文字时，就会发现他们的主要动机不只是去打破，而且是要创造表达其自体体验的新方式。他们几乎所有人都并不满足于冲突，而是渴望自己的作品获得专业与社会的认可。

In fact, it is interesting to note that for many of these artists, the failure to receive the requisite mirroring in response to their work, or the anticipation of selfobject failure, led to disastrous and even suicidal results. Once again, behind the revolutionary movement of abstract expressionism was a desire to express subjectivity more accurately, vividly, and powerfully. For these artists, the experience of the modern self required a new, more dramatic form of expression. Through the development of new approaches to technique and form, each abstract expressionist sought to create a sense of aesthetic resonance that would capture his or her sense of lived experience as accurately as possible.

事实上，值得注意的是，对这些艺术家中的许多人来说，其作品未能获得所需的镜像回应，或者对自体客体失败的预期，导致了灾难性甚至自杀性的后果。再一次地，在抽象表现主义这场革命运动的背后，是一种要更准确、更生动、更有力地表达主体性的愿望。对这些艺术家而言，现代自体的体验需要一种新的、更具戏剧性的表达形式。通过发展技法与形式上的新进路，每一位抽象表现主义者都试图创造一种审美共鸣感，以尽可能准确地捕捉其活生生经验之感。

Such a process is challenging and often stressful. Carol Press, in The Dancing Self (2002), emphasized the creative artist's need for strength, both from within and outside the self:

这样一个过程既具挑战性，又常常充满压力。卡罗尔·普雷斯[Carol Press]在《舞动的自体》[The Dancing Self]（2002）中强调，从事创造的艺术家需要力量——既来自自体内部，也来自自体之外：

The artist must possess enough psychological strength to destabilize, without overwhelming fragmentation, to venture into new realms and discoveries. The need for functioning selfobjects is paramount. The ability to find sustaining selfobject experiences is a sign of health. Artists must have the capacity to shore up their selves and find fulfillment from their selfobject needs as they progress through the significant and challenging phases of creativity. (p. 100)

艺术家必须拥有足够的心理力量，使自己能够失去稳定（而不至于陷入压倒性的碎裂），从而敢于闯入新的领域与新的发现。对能够发挥功能的自体客体[functioning selfobjects]的需要是首要的。能够找到支持性的自体客体体验，是健康的标志。艺术家必须有能力支撑自己的自体，并在穿越创造力那些重要而充满挑战的阶段时，从自己的自体客体需要中获得满足。 （p. 100）
