DRAFT J² · FRAU P. J.（27 岁）

〔I〕

She had been married for three months. Her husband, a commercial traveller, had had to leave her a few weeks after their marriage and had already been away for weeks on end. She missed him very much and longed for him. She had been a singer, or at any rate had been trained as one. To pass the time, she was sitting at the piano singing, when suddenly she felt ill – in her abdomen and stomach, her head swam, she had feelings of oppression and anxiety and cardiac paraesthesia; she thought she was going mad. A moment later it occurred to her that she had eaten eggs and mushrooms that morning, and she concluded that she had been poisoned. However, the condition quickly passed off. Next day the maidservant told her that a woman living in the same house had gone mad. From that time on she was never free of an obsession [Zwangsidée], accompanied by anxiety, that she was going to go mad too.

她结婚三个月了。她的丈夫是个行商，婚后数周便不得不离开她，此后已一连数周在外。她非常想念他、渴慕他。她曾是一名歌手，至少受过歌唱训练。为打发时间，她坐在钢琴前唱歌，忽然感到不适——在腹部与胃部，头昏，有压迫感与焦虑，还有心脏的感觉异常[cardiac paraesthesia]；她以为自己要发疯了。片刻之后她想起，那天早上她吃过鸡蛋和蘑菇，于是断定自己中了毒。然而这状况很快过去了。第二天女仆告诉她，同住一屋的一个女人发了疯。从那时起，她便再没有摆脱过一个强迫观念[Zwangsidée]ᵀ——伴随焦虑：她也要发疯了。

Such is the outline of the case. I assumed to start with that her condition then had been an anxiety attack – a release of sexual feeling which was transformed into anxiety. An attack of that kind, I was afraid, might take place without any accompanying psychical process. Nevertheless, I would not reject the more favourable possibility that such a process might be found; on the contrary, I would take it as the starting point of my work. What I expected to find was this. She had had a longing for her husband – that is, for sexual relations with him; she had thus come upon an idea which had excited sexual affect and afterwards defence against the idea; she had then taken fright and made a false connection or substitution.¹

这就是病例的轮廓。我一开始便假定：她当时的状况是一次焦虑发作——一种性感受的释放，它被转变成了焦虑。我所担心的是，这类发作可能没有任何伴随的心理过程而发生。然而，我不愿排除较为有利的可能，即这样一个过程是可以找到的；相反，我要把它当作工作的起点。我所期待发现的是这样：她曾渴慕她的丈夫——也就是说，渴慕与他的性关系；于是她遇到了一个观念，这观念激起了性的情动，随后又引起对这观念的防御；接着她惊恐起来，并作出了一种虚假联系[false connection] 或替代。¹

I began by asking her about the subsidiary circumstances of the event: something must have reminded her of her husband. She had been singing Carmen’s aria ‘Près des remparts de Séville’.² I asked her to repeat it for me; she could not even recall the words exactly. – At what point do you think the attack came on? – She did not know. – When I applied pressure [to her forehead],³ she said it had been after she had finished the aria. That seemed quite possible: it had been a train of thought brought up by the text of the aria. – I then asserted that before the attack there had been thoughts present in her which she might not remember. In fact she remembered nothing, but pressure [on her forehead] produced ‘husband’ and ‘longing’. The latter was further specified, on my insistence, as longing for sexual caresses. – ‘I’m quite ready to believe that. After all, your attack was only a state of outpouring of love. Do you know the page’s song?’

我先询问这件事的附带情况：必有什么使她想起了丈夫。她当时在唱卡门[Carmen] 的咏叹调《在塞维利亚的城墙边》[Près des remparts de Séville]。² 我请她为我重唱一遍；她连歌词都记不确切了。——你认为发作是在哪一刻发生的？——她不知道。——当我〔在她的额头〕施加压力时，³ 她说是在她唱完咏叹调之后。这看来颇有可能：那是一串由咏叹调的歌词引出的思绪。——于是我断言：在发作之前，她心中曾有她也许并不记得的念头。事实上她什么也不记得，但〔额头的〕压力产生了『丈夫』与『渴慕』。在我的坚持下，后者被进一步明确为对性爱抚的渴慕。——『我很愿意相信这一点。毕竟，你的发作只是一种爱之涌流的状态。你知道那侍童唱的歌吗？』

Voi che sapete che cosa è amor,
Donne vedete s’io l’ho nel cor . . .⁴

『你们知道爱情是什么，
女士们，请看它是否在我心中……』⁴
（原词为意大利文，出自莫扎特《费加罗的婚礼》第二幕凯鲁比诺的小咏叹调。）

There was certainly something besides this: a feeling in the lower part of the body, a cramp and an urgent need to urinate.’ – She now conﬁrmed this. The insincerity of women starts from their omitting the characteristic sexual symptoms in describing their states. So it had really been an orgasm.

『此外肯定还有别的：身体下部的感觉，一阵抽紧，以及急于排尿的需要。』——她这时确认了这一点。女人的不坦诚，始于她们在描述自己的状态时略去那些特征性的性症状。所以那确实是一次情欲高潮[orgasm]。

‘Well you can see, anyhow, that a state of longing like that in a young woman who has been left by her husband cannot be anything to be ashamed of.’ – On the contrary, she thought, that is how it should be.¹ – ‘Very well; but in that case I can see no reason for fright. You were certainly not frightened about “husband” and “longing”; so there must be some other thoughts missing, which are more appropriate to the fright.’ – But she only added that she had all along been afraid of the pains that intercourse caused her, but that her longing had been much stronger than her fear of the pains. – At this point we broke off.

『无论如何你总看得出：一个被丈夫留下的年轻女子，这样的渴慕状态不可能有什么可羞耻的。』——恰恰相反，她认为本来就该如此。¹——『很好；但既然如此，我看不出惊恐有什么理由。你肯定不是为「丈夫」和「渴慕」而惊恐；那么必定还缺了另一些念头，它们才更适合于这惊恐。』——但她只是补充说：她一向害怕性交给她的疼痛，而她的渴慕远强于她对疼痛的恐惧。——说到这里，我们中断了。

〔II〕

It was much to be suspected that in Scene I (by the piano), alongside of her longing thoughts for her husband (which she remembered), she had entered on another, deep-going train of thought, which she did not remember and which led to a Scene II. But I still did not as yet know its starting point. Today she arrived weeping and in despair, evidently without any hope of the treatment succeeding. So her resistance was already stirred up and progress was far more diffcult. What I wanted to know, then, was what thoughts might lead to her being frightened were still present. She brought up all kinds of things that could not be relevant: the fact that for a long time she had not been deflowered (which Professor Chrobak² had conﬁrmed to her), that she attributed her nervous state to that and for that reason wished it might be done. – This was, of course, a thought from a later time: till Scene I she had been in good health. – At last I obtained the information that she had already had a similar, but much weaker and more transitory, attack with the same feelings. (From this I saw that it was from the mnemic picture of the orgasm itself that the path leading down to the deeper layers took its start.) We investigated the other scene. At that time – four years back – she had had an engagement at Ratisbon. In the morning she had sung at a rehearsal and had given satisfaction. In the afternoon, at home, she had had a ‘vision’ – as if there were something, a ‘row’, between her and the tenor of the company and another man, and afterwards she had had the attack, with the fear that she was going mad.

极可怀疑的是：在第一幕（钢琴旁），除了她对丈夫的渴慕念头（这些她记得）之外，她还进入了另一条深入下去的思绪——她不记得它，而它通向第二幕。但我当时还不知道它的起点。今天她哭着、绝望地来了，显然对治疗能否成功不抱希望。可见她的抵抗已经被激起，进展也困难得多。于是我得知的是：那些可能引她惊恐的念头，是否仍然存在。她搬出种种不可能相关的说法：她长久以来并未被破贞（克罗巴克[Chrobak] 教授² 曾向她确认这一点），她把自己的神经质状态归因于此，并因此希望这件事能被完成。——当然，这是一个来自较晚时点的念头：直到第一幕之前，她一直健康。——最后我得到了这样一个信息：她此前已有过一次类似但轻微得多、短暂得多的发作，伴有同样的感觉。（由此我看出：正是从情欲高潮的记忆图像出发，那条通向下层的路才得以开始。）我们考察了另一幕。那时——四年前——她在拉蒂斯本[Ratisbon] 有过一次聘约。早上她在排练中唱过，令人满意。下午在家时，她有过一个『幻视』[vision]——仿佛她与剧团里的男高音以及另一个男人之间，出了什么事、有一场『争吵』；随后她就有过那次发作，伴随着自己会发疯的恐惧。

Here then was a Scene II, which had been touched on by association in Scene I. But it was evident that here, too, the memory had gaps in it. There must have been other ideas present in order to account for the release of sexual feeling and the fright. I asked for these intermediate links, but instead I was told her motives [Motive]ᵀ. whole of life on the stage. – ‘Why?’ – The brusqueness of the manager and the actors’ relations to one another. – I asked for details of this. – There had been an old comic actress with whom the young men used to joke by asking her if they might come and spend the night with her. – ‘Something further, about the tenor.’ – He had pestered her, too; at the rehearsal he had put his hand on her breast. – ‘Through your clothes or on the bare skin?’ – At first she said the latter, but then took it back: she had been in outdoor clothes. – ‘Well, what more?’ – The whole character of their relations, all the hugging and kissing among the players had been frightful to her. – ‘Yes?’ – Once again the manager’s brusqueness, and she had only stayed there a few days. – ‘Was the tenor’s assault made on the same day as your attack?’ – No; she did not know if it had been earlier or later. – My enquiries with the help of pressure showed that the assault had been on the fourth day of her stay and her attack on the sixth.

整个舞台生涯。——『为什么？』——经理的粗暴，以及演员彼此之间的关系。——我请她说得具体些。——曾有一位年老的喜剧女演员，年轻男子们常拿她开玩笑，问她能否让他们来与她过夜。——『还有别的，关于那男高音。』——他也纠缠过她；在排练时他把手放在她的胸上。——『隔着衣服，还是直接碰到皮肤？』——起初她说后者，随即又收回：她当时穿着外出服。——『还有呢？』——他们交往的整个性质，演员之间所有的拥抱接吻，都令她恐惧。——『是吗？』——又一次是经理的粗暴；而她只在那里待了几天。——『男高音的侵犯与你的发作是同一天吗？』——不；她不知道是更早还是更晚。——我借助压力的探询表明：那次侵犯发生在她逗留的第四天，她的发作在第六天。
